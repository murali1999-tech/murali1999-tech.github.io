Cyclospora Outbreak in the United States

Cyclospora is receiving significant attention in the United States because of a large 2026 outbreak of cyclosporiasis, an intestinal illness caused by the parasite Cyclospora cayetanensis. Federal health agencies are continuing to investigate the outbreak and its connection with contaminated iceberg lettuce.

Latest CDC figure: Since May 1, 2026, the CDC has received reports of 15,716 laboratory-confirmed cases of cyclosporiasis acquired in the United States. The CDC says additional cases may require further investigation.

The current outbreak investigation has focused on iceberg lettuce sourced from central Mexico and recalled by Taylor Farms de Mexico. The FDA and CDC have been working with state and local health authorities to identify illnesses and trace potentially contaminated food through the supply chain.

The FDA's August 20 update said the specific multistate outbreak linked to the recalled iceberg lettuce had reached 10,930 illnesses in 17 states, including 454 hospitalizations and two deaths. The agency said its investigation remains ongoing.

The recalled lettuce was distributed to food-service businesses and retailers across numerous states. The FDA says the recalled products should no longer be available for sale and that consumers should follow food-safety instructions concerning recalled products.

What Is Cyclospora?

Cyclospora cayetanensis is a parasite that can infect the digestive system. Infection is known as cyclosporiasis. People can become infected by consuming food or water contaminated with the parasite.

Common symptoms can include prolonged watery diarrhea, stomach cramps, bloating, increased gas, nausea, fatigue and loss of appetite. Weight loss can also occur. Some infected people may not develop noticeable symptoms.

The FDA notes that untreated illness can lead to dehydration and complications, and symptoms can sometimes disappear and return. People experiencing persistent symptoms should contact a healthcare professional.

Food-safety reminder: If you believe you consumed a recalled food and later developed symptoms consistent with cyclosporiasis, contact a healthcare professional and mention the possible food exposure.

Consumers should check current FDA and CDC information rather than relying on social-media posts or outdated recall lists because investigations and product distribution information can change.

Iceberg Lettuce Recall and Cyclospora Investigation

Federal investigators have linked the specific multistate outbreak to iceberg lettuce sourced from central Mexico. Taylor Farms de Mexico recalled the affected iceberg lettuce in July 2026.

The FDA says recalled products should no longer be available in stores or restaurants. The agency has also conducted inspections and sampling at lettuce growers in Mexico while working with Mexican officials.

The investigation demonstrates why food traceability is important during large outbreaks. Health officials need to identify a common source quickly so potentially contaminated products can be removed from the market.

The current outbreak has also prompted renewed attention to food-safety systems, supply-chain tracking and the handling of fresh produce.

Official information can be found through the FDA Cyclospora investigation and the CDC Cyclospora surveillance page.

El Niño Winter 2026-27 Temperature and Precipitation Outlook

Another major topic being watched across the United States is the strengthening El Niño pattern and its possible influence on winter temperature and precipitation.

NOAA's Climate Prediction Center issued an El Niño Advisory in August 2026. The agency said El Niño is strengthening and there is a greater than 90% chance of a very strong event during the Northern Hemisphere fall and winter of 2026-27.

NOAA outlook: El Niño is expected to influence U.S. temperature and precipitation patterns through the 2026-27 winter, although local conditions can vary considerably.

Seasonal outlooks are not the same as a daily weather forecast. They describe probabilities of temperatures and precipitation being above, near or below normal over a broad period. Individual storms and cold spells can still occur even during a warmer overall winter pattern.

U.S. Winter Temperature

NOAA's seasonal outlook has favored above-normal temperatures across broad portions of the contiguous United States and Alaska. The probabilities can change as the season approaches and as atmospheric and ocean conditions evolve.

U.S. Winter Precipitation

El Niño typically changes the winter jet stream and precipitation pattern across the United States. NOAA's long-lead outlook indicates a developing El Niño-like precipitation pattern, with wetter conditions favored across parts of the southern tier and drier conditions favored in portions of the Northwest and north-central United States during parts of the winter.

The exact temperature and precipitation outcome will differ by region. Residents should use NOAA's updated seasonal forecasts and local National Weather Service forecasts for location-specific information.

Why El Niño Matters for the United States

El Niño develops when sea-surface temperatures in the central and eastern tropical Pacific become warmer than normal and interact with changes in atmospheric circulation.

These changes can affect the position of the jet stream and storm tracks. Historically, El Niño winters have been associated with increased precipitation across parts of the southern United States and different precipitation patterns in the northern tier.

The strongest effects are not identical every year. Other atmospheric patterns can strengthen, weaken or shift the typical El Niño influence.

The 2026-27 event is attracting particular attention because NOAA currently expects a very strong El Niño during the Northern Hemisphere fall and winter.
