Jobs Report Today: August 2026 Jobs Report and Key Numbers

The latest U.S. jobs report is out today, September 4, 2026, with the Bureau of Labor Statistics reporting that nonfarm payroll employment increased by 162,000 in August. The unemployment rate remained unchanged at 4.1%. The August employment report was released at 8:30 a.m. Eastern Time and is one of the most closely watched economic reports for financial markets, businesses and workers.

The August jobs report provides a snapshot of the U.S. labor market and includes employment, unemployment, wages, hours worked and changes across major industries. The report is particularly important for investors because labor-market conditions can influence expectations for Federal Reserve monetary policy, interest rates, the U.S. dollar and financial markets.

According to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, the 162,000 increase in nonfarm payrolls was substantially stronger than the average monthly gain of 31,000 during the previous 12 months. The unemployment rate stayed at 4.1%, while the number of unemployed people was approximately 7.0 million.

The August employment increase was concentrated in several areas. Food services and drinking places added 59,000 jobs, while local government education employment increased by 42,000. Manufacturing employment continued to trend upward, adding 16,000 jobs during the month.

Health care also continued to add jobs, although the pace was slower than its recent average. The sector added 13,000 positions in August, including gains in home health care services and hospitals. Construction employment changed little overall, with a reported increase of 22,000 jobs.

One of the weaker areas was the information industry. Employment in information declined by 23,000 in August. The Bureau of Labor Statistics said losses included computing infrastructure providers, data processing, web hosting and related services, as well as publishing and broadcasting and content providers.

The latest jobs report also contained revisions to previous months. June payroll employment was revised from an initial increase of 20,000 to an increase of 31,000. July was revised from a decline of 23,000 to an increase of 21,000. Together, employment in June and July was revised 55,000 higher than previously reported.

Wages also increased in August. Average hourly earnings for private nonfarm employees rose by 10 cents, or 0.3%, to $37.75. Over the year, average hourly earnings increased by 3.1%. For private-sector production and nonsupervisory employees, average hourly earnings increased by 11 cents to $32.53.

The average workweek also edged higher. The average workweek for private nonfarm employees increased by 0.1 hour to 34.4 hours. Manufacturing employees worked an average of 40.5 hours, with the manufacturing workweek also increasing slightly during August.

The labor force participation rate edged up to 61.6% in August. However, the rate is still 0.5 percentage point below its January level. The employment-population ratio remained at 59.1%.

The number of people working part time for economic reasons fell by 414,000 to 4.4 million. These are people who wanted full-time employment but were working part time because their hours had been reduced or because they could not find a full-time job.

The number of long-term unemployed people, defined by the BLS as people unemployed for 27 weeks or more, remained around 1.9 million. Long-term unemployed people represented 27.0% of all unemployed people in August.

The jobs report is closely followed by financial traders. Search interest often increases for terms such as jobs report today, August jobs report, job report, CNBC jobs report and Forex Factory jobs data immediately before and after the release. Traders commonly compare the actual payroll number and unemployment rate with market expectations.

It is important to remember that websites such as CNBC and Forex Factory are secondary sources for financial-market coverage. The Bureau of Labor Statistics is the primary source for the official U.S. employment data. The BLS publishes the Employment Situation report and provides the underlying tables used to analyze employment, unemployment and wages.

The jobs report can also influence expectations about the Federal Reserve. A stronger labor market can potentially reduce expectations for immediate interest-rate cuts, while a weaker labor market can increase expectations that policymakers may need to provide more support to economic activity. However, employment data is only one part of the Federal Reserve's decision-making process.

The August figures therefore provide a mixed but generally stronger picture than the recent payroll trend alone might suggest. Payroll employment increased by 162,000, significantly above the prior 12-month average, while unemployment stayed at 4.1%. At the same time, information-sector employment declined and health-care job growth was slower than its recent average.

For workers and job seekers, the headline payroll number does not tell the entire story. Industry-level changes can be more important depending on the type of work a person is seeking. Food services, local government education and manufacturing recorded gains in August, while information employment declined.

For investors, the next steps will include watching how markets interpret the employment numbers alongside inflation, consumer spending and other economic indicators. The wage-growth figure of 3.1% over the year is another important number because labor costs and wage growth can influence inflation expectations.

The next monthly Employment Situation report is scheduled for October 2, 2026, at 8:30 a.m. Eastern Time and will cover September employment. Until then, the August report will remain an important reference point for assessing the direction of the U.S. labor market.

The official August 2026 jobs report shows that U.S. nonfarm payroll employment increased by 162,000 and unemployment remained at 4.1%. The report also shows gains in food services, local government education and manufacturing, while information-sector employment fell. Average hourly earnings increased 0.3% during the month and were up 3.1% from a year earlier.
