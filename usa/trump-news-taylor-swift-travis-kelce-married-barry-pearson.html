Former Steelers Player Barry Pearson

Barry Pearson is a former professional American football wide receiver who played for the Pittsburgh Steelers and Kansas City Chiefs during the 1970s. Pearson was born on February 4, 1950, in Geneseo, Illinois, and played college football at Northwestern.

Pearson entered the NFL in 1972 and spent the 1972 and 1973 seasons with the Pittsburgh Steelers before continuing his professional career with the Kansas City Chiefs. NFL records list him as a wide receiver and document his appearances and receiving statistics during his career.

Barry Pearson: Former Pittsburgh Steelers wide receiver who also played for the Kansas City Chiefs. He was part of the Steelers during the era of the famous 1972 Immaculate Reception.

Pearson has a particularly interesting connection to one of the most famous plays in NFL history. During the 1972 playoff game involving the Pittsburgh Steelers, the play that became known as the Immaculate Reception was originally designed with Pearson as the intended receiver. Terry Bradshaw ultimately threw the ball toward Frenchy Fuqua, leading to Franco Harris making the historic catch.

Pearson later continued his NFL career with Kansas City. His professional statistics include receiving production during his seasons with the Chiefs, and he remains part of the historical story surrounding the Steelers' early championship era.
