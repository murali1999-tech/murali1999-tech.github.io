Japan Tokyo Earthquake Today

A magnitude 5.9 earthquake struck eastern Japan early Sunday, August 23, shaking Tokyo and the wider Kanto region. The earthquake occurred at approximately 2:00 a.m. local time, with the epicenter in southern Ibaraki Prefecture at a depth of around 70 kilometers.

The earthquake registered lower 5 on Japan's seven-level seismic intensity scale in parts of Ibaraki. The shaking was widely felt across the Tokyo metropolitan region and triggered emergency earthquake alerts on mobile phones.

At least 37 people were reported injured across several prefectures, with most injuries described as minor. Transportation was also disrupted, including delays to some express trains serving Tokyo and Narita Airport.

No tsunami warning was issued following the earthquake. Nuclear facilities in the affected region also reported no abnormalities. Japanese authorities urged residents to remain cautious about possible aftershocks during the following week.

Tropical Storm Moke

Tropical Storm Moke is approaching Hawaii as the state continues to recover from Hurricane Lala. The storm is expected to bring heavy rainfall and strong winds, particularly to the Big Island.

Moke was moving west-northwest toward the Hawaiian Islands. Forecasts indicated that the Big Island could receive several inches of rain, with isolated areas potentially receiving significantly higher totals. The combination of heavy rainfall and already saturated ground increases the risk of flash flooding and mudslides.

Officials have urged residents and visitors to monitor weather alerts, prepare essential supplies and avoid flooded roads. Dangerous surf and rip currents can also become a concern when tropical systems pass near the islands.

The storm is expected to pass south of the main Hawaiian Islands rather than make a direct landfall, but even a near miss can produce substantial rainfall and strong winds.
