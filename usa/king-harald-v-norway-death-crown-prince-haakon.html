King Harald V of Norway Dies at 89: Crown Prince Haakon Next in Line

Published: August 28, 2026 | World News

King Harald V of Norway has died at the age of 89, according to an announcement from the Norwegian Royal Court. The Norwegian monarch died peacefully at Oslo University Hospital Rikshospitalet on Friday, August 28, 2026, at 6:35 a.m. The announcement marks the end of King Harald's long reign and begins a new chapter for the Norwegian royal family.

King Harald V had been Norway's monarch since January 1991, when he succeeded his father, King Olav V. During more than three decades on the throne, Harald became a familiar and respected figure in Norway, representing the country at official ceremonies, state occasions, international visits and important national events.

The Royal Court had announced on August 27 that King Harald's condition remained unchanged after his health had deteriorated. The palace had previously said that he was receiving treatment for a bloodstream bacterial infection and had responded to antibiotics. His death was subsequently announced by the Royal Court on August 28.

King Harald V was born on February 21, 1937, at Skaugum in Asker. He was the son of Crown Prince Olav and Crown Princess Märtha and the grandson of King Haakon VII. According to the Royal Court, Harald was the first prince born in Norway in 567 years.

His childhood was dramatically affected by the Second World War. After Germany invaded Norway in April 1940, the Norwegian Royal Family left the country. Harald spent part of the war years abroad before returning to Norway with his family following the liberation in 1945.

Harald became Crown Prince in 1957 following the death of his grandfather, King Haakon VII. He gradually took on more official responsibilities and served as regent when his father, King Olav V, was unable to perform his duties.

King Olav V died on January 17, 1991. At that moment Crown Prince Harald became King Harald V. He formally swore an oath to uphold the Norwegian Constitution in the Storting four days later. King Harald and Queen Sonja were consecrated in Nidaros Cathedral in Trondheim on June 23, 1991.

King Harald's official role was mainly representative and ceremonial, although the Norwegian Constitution gives the monarch several formal responsibilities. The King presided over the Council of State, took part in formal changes of government and formally opened the Norwegian Parliament each autumn.

King Harald was also Norway's highest-ranking military officer. He held the rank of General in the Army and Air Force and Admiral in the Navy. The Royal Court said that he maintained an active interest in the Norwegian Armed Forces and attended important military ceremonies and commemorations.

Sports and outdoor activities were among King Harald's well-known interests. He was particularly associated with sailing and had represented Norway in sailing competitions. He also enjoyed skiing, hunting and fishing and frequently spoke about his connection with nature and outdoor life.

King Harald married Sonja Haraldsen in 1968. She became Queen Sonja when Harald became king in 1991. Their marriage was significant in Norwegian royal history because Sonja came from a non-royal background. The couple had two children, Princess Märtha Louise and Crown Prince Haakon.

Crown Prince Haakon is the heir to the Norwegian throne. Born on July 20, 1973, he is the son of King Harald V and Queen Sonja. Haakon became Crown Prince when his father became king in 1991.

Crown Prince Haakon has already carried out many of the responsibilities associated with the Norwegian monarchy. He has represented Norway at home and abroad and has frequently stepped in for his father when King Harald was unable to carry out official duties.

The Royal Court had confirmed before King Harald's death that Crown Prince Haakon was serving as regent while the King was on sick leave. Haakon carried out official engagements in that role during the final days of King Harald's life.

Prince Haakon married Mette-Marit Tjessem Høiby at Oslo Cathedral on August 25, 2001. The couple have two children together, Princess Ingrid Alexandra and Prince Sverre Magnus. Crown Prince Haakon also has a stepson, Marius Borg Høiby.

Under Norway's constitutional succession rules, Crown Prince Haakon is first in line to succeed King Harald. Princess Ingrid Alexandra is next in line after her father. The Norwegian succession system gives priority to direct descendants, with older children taking precedence over younger children.

The death of King Harald therefore does not create uncertainty over who comes next in the Norwegian monarchy. Crown Prince Haakon is the heir to the throne and has extensive experience performing official duties on behalf of the King.

The Norwegian monarchy is a constitutional monarchy. The King is the country's formal head of state, while political power is exercised by the elected government and parliament. The monarch's role includes representing the nation, carrying out constitutional duties and serving as a symbol of national unity.

King Harald V's reign lasted more than 35 years. He became king at a time when European monarchies were changing rapidly and remained a prominent figure in Norwegian public life for decades. His long service included state visits, meetings with foreign leaders, national celebrations and visits to communities throughout Norway.

The Royal Court's announcement of King Harald's death has prompted a new period of mourning in Norway. His death is expected to lead to official ceremonies and a transition of royal responsibilities as the country prepares for the next reign.

King Harald V leaves behind a long legacy as Norway's monarch. His reign connected several generations of the Norwegian royal family, from his grandfather King Haakon VII and father King Olav V to his son Crown Prince Haakon and granddaughter Princess Ingrid Alexandra.

The next chapter of the Norwegian monarchy will now focus on Crown Prince Haakon and his future role as King of Norway. His years of preparation and experience as heir and regent are expected to be important as the royal family begins the transition following King Harald's death.
