Recall warning: The FDA says consumers should not eat, sell or serve recalled GreenWise Organic Frozen Whole Blueberries and Whole Mixed Berries because of potential E. coli O145:H28 contamination.

A major blueberry recall is affecting certain frozen GreenWise organic blueberry and mixed berry products sold through Publix stores in the United States. The recall is connected to an ongoing FDA investigation into an outbreak of E. coli O145:H28 infections linked to frozen blueberries.

The FDA says Publix recalled all lots of GreenWise Organic Whole Blueberries and Whole Mixed Berries on July 29, 2026. Consumers who purchased these frozen products are being advised to check their freezers and avoid eating recalled products.

What Is the Blueberry Recall 2026?

The blueberry recall involves GreenWise Organic Frozen Whole Blueberries and GreenWise Organic Whole Mixed Berries. The products were sold at Publix stores in multiple states and are being recalled because they may be contaminated with E. coli O145:H28.

The FDA's investigation remains ongoing. According to the agency, the outbreak investigation involves frozen blueberries supplied by Frutas y Hortalizas del Sur S.A., a company based in San Carlos, Chile.

The FDA reported 12 illnesses associated with the outbreak as of its July 30 update, including four hospitalizations. No deaths had been reported at that time. The agency continues to investigate the source and extent of the contamination.

Earlier GreenWise Blueberry Recall

Before the broader Publix recall, Frutas y Hortalizas del Sur S.A. announced a recall on July 3, 2026 involving GreenWise Organic IQF Frozen Blueberries in 10-ounce packages.

The earlier recall applied specifically to product with lot code 60401, printed on the package as 6 040 01, and a Best By date of February 9, 2028. The affected product was distributed to Publix stores in eight states.

The FDA later reported that Publix expanded the action to recall all lots of GreenWise Organic Whole Blueberries and Whole Mixed Berries because of the ongoing E. coli investigation.

Where Were the Recalled Blueberries Sold?

The recalled frozen GreenWise products were shipped to Publix retail stores in Alabama, Florida, Georgia, Kentucky, North Carolina, South Carolina, Tennessee and Virginia.

Consumers in these states who purchased frozen GreenWise blueberries or mixed berries should check their freezers carefully. The FDA also advises consumers who froze berries after removing the original packaging to discard them if they cannot determine whether the product is included in the recall.

Why Were the Blueberries Recalled?

The recall is related to possible contamination with E. coli O145:H28. This strain belongs to a group known as Shiga toxin-producing E. coli, or STEC.

E. coli infections can cause symptoms such as severe stomach cramps, diarrhea, fever, nausea and vomiting. Some infections can become serious, particularly when complications develop.

The FDA warns that certain E. coli infections can lead to hemolytic uremic syndrome, commonly called HUS. HUS can affect the kidneys and may require medical treatment.

What Should Consumers Do?

Consumers should not eat the recalled GreenWise Organic Frozen Whole Blueberries or Whole Mixed Berries. The FDA recommends throwing recalled products away or returning them to the place of purchase for a refund.

If a recalled product was stored in a freezer, consumers should also clean and sanitize surfaces or containers that came into contact with the berries. This can help reduce the risk of cross-contamination.

Consumers should not taste the berries to determine whether they are safe. Food contaminated with harmful bacteria may not have an unusual smell, appearance or taste.

What Are the Symptoms of E. coli Infection?

Symptoms associated with E. coli infection can include severe stomach cramps, diarrhea and vomiting. Diarrhea may sometimes become bloody. Fever and nausea can also occur.

According to the FDA, symptoms can begin a few days after consuming contaminated food or as long as nine days afterward. Anyone who develops concerning symptoms after consuming a recalled product should contact a healthcare professional.

Is Every Blueberry Recalled?

No. The recall does not mean that all fresh or frozen blueberries sold in the United States are contaminated. The FDA's advisory specifically identifies certain GreenWise frozen blueberry and mixed berry products connected to the investigation.

Consumers should check product names, package sizes and UPC information against the official recall information rather than assuming that every blueberry product is affected.

FDA Investigation Continues

The FDA says the investigation into the E. coli outbreak is ongoing. The agency and its state partners are working to determine the source of contamination and whether additional products need to be added to the recall.

The FDA has also conducted a regulatory assessment of the Chilean supplier and collected records to better understand the company's procedures and controls. Additional information may be released as the investigation continues.

The 2026 blueberry recall is specifically important for customers who purchased frozen GreenWise Organic Whole Blueberries or Whole Mixed Berries from Publix. Because Publix recalled all lots of the affected products, consumers should avoid eating the recalled items and follow the FDA's instructions for disposal or return.

The FDA continues to investigate the E. coli O145:H28 outbreak and could update its advisory if additional products or information become available. Consumers should check official FDA recall information for the latest guidance.
