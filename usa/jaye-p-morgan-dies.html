Jaye P. Morgan Dies at 94: Singer and Gong Show Star Remembered

Jaye P. Morgan, the singer, actress and television personality who became widely known as a celebrity panelist on The Gong Show, has died at the age of 94. Morgan died on August 24, 2026, according to reports and an obituary published after her passing.

Born Mary Margaret Morgan on December 3, 1931, in Mancos, Colorado, she began performing at a very young age. Her family was involved in entertainment, and Morgan eventually developed a successful career as a singer before becoming a familiar face on American television.

Morgan achieved considerable success as a recording artist during the 1950s. She had several hit songs during that period, including "That's All I Want From You," which became one of her best-known recordings. She also recorded "The Longest Walk," "Danger! Heartbreak Ahead," "If You Don't Want My Love" and "Pepper-Hot Baby."

Her career was not limited to music. Morgan appeared on television programs and became a frequent guest on variety and talk shows. Her personality and distinctive voice helped her remain recognizable as entertainment tastes changed over the decades.

For many viewers, however, Jaye P. Morgan is most closely associated with The Gong Show. She served as one of the show's judges during its original run in the 1970s. The program was known for its unusual performers, comedy and unpredictable moments, and Morgan quickly became one of its most recognizable personalities.

Her time on The Gong Show also produced one of the most talked-about moments of her television career. During a performance, Morgan exposed her breasts on live television. The incident became part of the show's history and added to her reputation for being unpredictable and willing to play along with its unusual style.

Outside The Gong Show, Morgan appeared on programs including The Tonight Show Starring Johnny Carson, The Merv Griffin Show, Match Game and Hollywood Squares. She also appeared in films and made a later appearance as herself in the 2003 movie Confessions of a Dangerous Mind.

Reports about her death have confirmed that Morgan died in Washington state. Her obituary listed Castle Rock, Washington, as her residence. The Associated Press reported that she died of natural causes in Longview, Washington, according to Mark Sendroff, her longtime attorney and friend.

News of Morgan's death has brought renewed attention to her long career in American entertainment. She successfully moved between music, television and comedy at a time when performers often built careers across several different forms of entertainment.

Jaye P. Morgan leaves behind a career that stretched across decades and included hit records, television appearances and one of the most memorable judging roles in classic game-show history. She was 94.
