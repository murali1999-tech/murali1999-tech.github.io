Brian Kelly CBS Sports Debut: Former LSU and Notre Dame Coach Enters the Broadcast Booth

Brian Kelly has officially moved from the college football sideline to the broadcast booth. The former Notre Dame and LSU head coach made his CBS Sports debut on August 29, 2026, joining the network's coverage of the Jacksonville State at North Dakota State game on CBS Sports Network.

The appearance marked a major change for Kelly. For more than four decades, he had started football seasons as a coach. His first coaching job came in 1983, and he eventually built a career that included Grand Valley State, Central Michigan, Cincinnati, Notre Dame and LSU. In 2026, for the first time in his coaching career, he began a college football season as an analyst instead of a head coach.

Kelly's first CBS Sports assignment was the Week 0 matchup between Jacksonville State and North Dakota State at the Fargodome in Fargo, North Dakota. He worked in the broadcast booth with play-by-play announcer Rich Waltz and analyst Adam Breneman.

The debut gave viewers an opportunity to hear Kelly discuss football from a different perspective. Instead of calling plays from the sideline, he was explaining what was happening on the field and providing observations based on his experience as a longtime head coach.

Kelly entered broadcasting after his four-season tenure at LSU ended during the 2025 season. LSU dismissed him after the Tigers started the season 5-3. Kelly finished his LSU tenure with a 34-14 record over four seasons. Lane Kiffin subsequently became LSU's head coach.

Before LSU, Kelly spent 12 seasons at Notre Dame from 2010 through 2021. He became the winningest coach in Notre Dame football history during his time with the Fighting Irish and guided the program to major postseason success. His Notre Dame tenure included an appearance in the 2012 BCS National Championship Game and two College Football Playoff appearances.

His move to CBS Sports was announced officially in July 2026. CBS said Kelly would work as an analyst on select college football games, appear as a studio analyst on Inside College Football on Tuesdays and contribute to CBS Sports HQ.

Kelly had already received some experience in front of the camera before his first game assignment. He worked with CBS Sports Network around the 2026 NFL Draft, giving him an introduction to television analysis before moving into live college football broadcasts.

The August 29 game was therefore an important test. Kelly had to adapt from preparing a team during the week to preparing for a broadcast. His job was no longer to design a game plan or manage players. Instead, he had to explain formations, decisions, matchups and strategy to viewers while the game was taking place.

After the broadcast, Kelly was candid about his performance. He acknowledged that he still had room to improve and thanked the CBS team for making his first experience easier than he expected. That reaction was notable because Kelly had spent most of his professional life evaluating players and teams rather than evaluating himself as a television analyst.

One of the interesting parts of Kelly's new role is the perspective he brings from the sideline. He has coached hundreds of college football games and has dealt with recruiting, roster construction, game preparation, player development and conference competition. Those experiences can give him a different perspective from analysts who have spent their careers primarily in broadcasting.

His knowledge of Notre Dame and LSU also gives him an obvious connection to two of the most closely followed programs in college football. Kelly has already provided CBS Sports with predictions and analysis concerning both programs ahead of the 2026 season.

In August, Kelly offered game-by-game predictions for LSU and Notre Dame. He discussed the challenges facing Lane Kiffin in his first season at LSU and also gave his assessment of Notre Dame's prospects. His predictions generated additional attention because of his personal history with both programs.

Kelly's CBS role does not necessarily mean that his coaching career is finished forever. He has previously indicated that he remains open to returning to coaching. For now, however, his focus is on broadcasting and remaining closely connected to college football.

The 2026 season gives Kelly an opportunity to see the sport from the other side. He can watch coaches make decisions, evaluate players and analyze teams without carrying responsibility for the final result. That may also give him an opportunity to observe how college football has changed since his departure from LSU.

The sport has changed significantly during Kelly's coaching career. The transfer portal, name, image and likeness opportunities, expanded player movement and changes in conference structures have transformed roster management. Kelly has frequently discussed the changing college football environment, making his perspective particularly relevant as a broadcaster.

His debut also comes at an interesting time for CBS Sports. The network has expanded and adjusted its college football coverage for the 2026 season, and Kelly joins a group of analysts with extensive coaching and playing experience.

For fans, the biggest question is how quickly Kelly will become comfortable in his new role. Coaching and broadcasting require different skills. A coach can communicate directly with players and staff, while a broadcaster has to explain complicated football concepts clearly and quickly to a large television audience.

Kelly's first game provided the beginning of that transition. His coaching background means that he already understands the technical side of the sport. The challenge will be turning that knowledge into clear, timely and entertaining television analysis.

There is also considerable curiosity about Kelly's future assignments. CBS Sports announced that he would work select games during the season, and he is also expected to appear on studio programming. His scheduled work includes additional college football broadcasts as the season continues.

His first CBS Sports appearance therefore represents more than a single broadcast. It is the beginning of a new professional chapter for a coach who spent decades on the sideline. After leaving LSU in 2025, Kelly has found a way to remain directly involved in college football while exploring what comes next.

Brian Kelly's CBS Sports debut took place on August 29, 2026, during Jacksonville State's game at North Dakota State on CBS Sports Network. He worked with Rich Waltz and Adam Breneman and brought his extensive coaching experience into the booth.

For a coach accustomed to wearing a headset on the sideline, the microphone in the broadcast booth represents a very different responsibility. But Kelly's long experience gives him a strong foundation, and the 2026 season will show how he develops into a full-time television football analyst.

His first broadcast is now another milestone in a career that has already included national championships at the Division II level, major conference success, Notre Dame's College Football Playoff era and four seasons at LSU. In 2026, Brian Kelly has added another role to that résumé: CBS Sports college football analyst.
