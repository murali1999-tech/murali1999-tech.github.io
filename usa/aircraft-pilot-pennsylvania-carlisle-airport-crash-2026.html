Latest update: August 20, 2026

The aircraft pilot involved in a deadly aviation accident in Pennsylvania has become the focus of a developing investigation after a small airplane collided with a Pennsylvania State Police helicopter near Carlisle Airport. The crash happened on Wednesday evening, August 19, 2026, and resulted in one death and injuries to two state police aviation personnel.

According to authorities and early reports, the aircraft involved was a Cessna 150, while the other aircraft was a Bell 407 helicopter operated by the Pennsylvania State Police. The Federal Aviation Administration and National Transportation Safety Board are investigating the circumstances surrounding the collision.

What Happened to the Aircraft Pilot?

The pilot of the small Cessna 150 died in the crash. Authorities initially withheld the pilot's identity while officials worked to notify the person's next of kin. Later reporting identified the deceased pilot as Paul Sokoloski, 57, of Hershey, Pennsylvania.

The aircraft was reportedly approaching the airport area when it veered toward the helicopter. Investigators are examining the exact sequence of events, including aircraft positioning, communications and the actions of the pilots immediately before the collision.

Carlisle Airport Collision

The accident occurred at Carlisle Airport in Cumberland County, Pennsylvania, around the evening of August 19. The Pennsylvania State Police helicopter was conducting a routine training exercise near the runway when the Cessna became involved in the collision.

Officials described the crash as extremely violent. The Cessna was destroyed in the impact, while the Bell 407 helicopter also sustained major damage and came down near the airport runway. Emergency crews quickly responded to the scene.

Two Pennsylvania State Police Pilots Injured

Two Pennsylvania State Police personnel aboard the Bell 407 were injured. Authorities said they were able to escape the damaged helicopter and were transported for medical treatment. Their conditions were described as stable, with the injuries reported as non-life-threatening.

The incident could have resulted in additional fatalities because the helicopter was being operated as part of a police aviation training exercise. Officials credited the rapid response of emergency personnel and the actions of those involved in the rescue.

FAA and NTSB Investigation

The Federal Aviation Administration and National Transportation Safety Board are investigating the crash. Investigators will examine aircraft records, flight paths, communications, weather conditions, airport operations and other evidence to determine what caused the collision.

Authorities have not announced a final cause. Early descriptions of the incident should therefore be treated as preliminary. Aviation accident investigations can take considerable time because investigators need to reconstruct the movements of both aircraft and examine physical evidence from the crash site.

Why the Investigation Matters

The crash has raised questions about aircraft movements around airports without traditional control towers. Carlisle Airport uses common traffic radio communications, and investigators are expected to review communications made before the collision.

Understanding how the two aircraft approached one another will be an important part of the investigation. Investigators will also determine whether established procedures were followed and whether any communication, visibility or operational factors contributed to the accident.

Aircraft Pilot and Aviation Safety

Small aircraft such as the Cessna 150 are widely associated with general aviation and pilot training. Although these aircraft are designed for flight instruction and recreational aviation, pilots must constantly monitor surrounding traffic, runway activity and radio communications.

Airports can become particularly challenging environments when multiple aircraft are operating simultaneously. Pilots must maintain situational awareness during takeoff, landing, taxiing and low-altitude operations. Helicopter operations can add another layer of complexity because helicopters may hover, move laterally and operate close to runways or other airport areas.

What Happens Next?

Investigators will continue collecting evidence from the crash site and reviewing available information. The investigation could eventually produce a preliminary report followed by additional findings as more evidence is analyzed.

For now, authorities have not announced a final determination about responsibility or the precise cause of the collision. Further information may emerge as the FAA, NTSB and Pennsylvania authorities complete their investigation.
