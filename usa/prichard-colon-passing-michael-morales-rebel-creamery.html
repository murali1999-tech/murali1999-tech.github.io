Prichard Colón Passing

Former professional boxer Prichard Colón died on August 13, 2026, at the age of 33, more than a decade after suffering a catastrophic brain injury during a 2015 boxing match against Terrel Williams.

Colón's father and former trainer, Richard Colón, announced his son's death. The Puerto Rican boxer had suffered severe neurological damage following the 2015 bout and required emergency surgery. He subsequently spent 221 days in a coma. His death has prompted tributes from across the boxing community.

Before the injury, Colón was regarded as a promising young professional boxer. He entered his final bout with an undefeated professional record of 16 wins and 13 knockouts. His career was permanently changed by the injuries suffered during the fight.

Reports say Colón suffered a subdural hematoma after the bout. He eventually left hospital but remained severely disabled and required extensive care for the remainder of his life. His story became one of the most widely discussed examples of the dangers of traumatic brain injury in boxing.

What Happened to Prichard Colón?

On October 17, 2015, Colón fought Terrel Williams in Fairfax, Virginia. During the bout, Colón sustained repeated blows to the back of the head, which are illegal in boxing. He later became seriously ill after the fight and was diagnosed with a brain injury.

He underwent emergency brain surgery and entered a coma that lasted 221 days. After awakening, he faced profound physical and neurological disabilities. His family continued to care for him for many years.

The tragic story has renewed discussion about boxing safety, officiating, medical supervision and the need for strict enforcement of rules designed to protect fighters.
