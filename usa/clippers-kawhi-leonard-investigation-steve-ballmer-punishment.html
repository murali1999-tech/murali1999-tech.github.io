Clippers Punishment: Kawhi Leonard Investigation, Steve Ballmer Suspension and Draft Picks

The Los Angeles Clippers are facing one of the most significant penalties in recent NBA history after the league completed its investigation into salary-cap circumvention involving Kawhi Leonard and the organization. The NBA announced its findings on September 2, 2026, bringing an investigation that had lasted nearly a year to a dramatic conclusion.

The punishment includes five forfeited first-round draft picks, a $30 million fine for the Clippers and a one-year suspension for owner Steve Ballmer. Kawhi Leonard was also ordered to pay $700,000 to the league. Clippers executives Lawrence Frank and Gillian Zucker were separately suspended.

The investigation centered on allegations that the Clippers helped Leonard obtain off-court income opportunities that violated the NBA's salary-cap circumvention rules. According to the NBA's official findings, the organization facilitated endorsement arrangements involving Leonard and companies that had business relationships with the Clippers.

The league said the investigation found that the Clippers initiated off-court income opportunities involving Leonard and four companies: Aspiration Partners, Boingo Wireless, Daktronics and Lockton Insurance. The NBA concluded that the organization helped facilitate endorsement agreements and that business opportunities involving the team were used in connection with those arrangements.

The case first became widely known after journalist Pablo Torre reported on a previously undisclosed endorsement arrangement involving Leonard and Aspiration. Torre's reporting brought renewed attention to the relationship between Leonard, his representatives and the Clippers.

Aspiration later became an important part of the NBA investigation. The company eventually went bankrupt, adding another layer to a case that already involved a large number of financial documents and business relationships. The NBA commissioned an independent investigation by the law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz.

One of the biggest questions for Clippers fans is what did the Clippers do? The NBA says the organization helped arrange endorsement opportunities for Leonard, offered or facilitated business relationships with companies that worked with the team, and paid certain personal expenses for Leonard and people connected with him. The league determined that these actions violated its rules.

Steve Ballmer received the most severe individual punishment among the Clippers' leadership. The NBA suspended Ballmer from all league and team activities for one year. The league said Ballmer knowingly sought to help Leonard obtain off-court income opportunities and approved a business deal that it considered connected to an impermissible endorsement arrangement.

Ballmer has owned the Clippers since 2014 and has transformed the organization financially, including the construction and opening of the Intuit Dome. His suspension represents a major setback for the franchise's leadership at a time when the Clippers were attempting to establish a long-term future around their star players.

Lawrence Frank, the Clippers' president of basketball operations, was suspended without pay for six months. The NBA said Frank was involved in the impermissible endorsement arrangements and approved expenses involving Leonard and his family that violated league rules.

Gillian Zucker, the Clippers' president of business operations, received a one-year suspension without pay. The NBA said she was directly involved in the endorsement arrangements and provided false or misleading information to investigators.

Kawhi Leonard's punishment is different from the sanctions imposed on the organization. Leonard was ordered to pay $700,000. The NBA said Leonard, through conduct involving his former business manager Dennis Robertson, pressured the Clippers to help him obtain off-court income opportunities and failed to reimburse certain payments.

Leonard has not been suspended by the NBA, and his Clippers contract was not voided as part of the announced punishment. That distinction is important because many fans have been asking whether Kawhi Leonard will be banned or whether his NBA career could be affected by the investigation.

Dennis Robertson, Leonard's uncle and former business representative, was also punished. The NBA banned Robertson from conducting business or otherwise engaging with NBA teams and their affiliates on behalf of players, employees or other league and team personnel for five years.

The draft-pick penalty may have the biggest long-term effect on the Clippers. The team must forfeit its first-round selections in the 2029, 2030, 2031, 2032 and 2033 NBA drafts. Losing five future first-round picks significantly reduces the franchise's flexibility when building its roster.

Those draft picks are especially important because NBA teams rely on first-round selections to acquire young talent, make trades and create financial flexibility. The Clippers now have fewer assets available for future roster changes.

The NBA also imposed a five-year compliance and monitoring program on the Clippers organization and its personnel. The league said the program will be overseen by the NBA office and is designed to ensure that the organization follows league rules in the future.

The Clippers strongly rejected the NBA's findings. In a statement following the announcement, the organization said it believed the investigation was biased and argued that what the league communicated privately differed from what was announced publicly. The Clippers said they intend to challenge the penalties through the appropriate process.

The situation has also raised questions about a possible Kawhi Leonard trade. Reports have indicated that Leonard's future became linked to the investigation because the league wanted the case resolved before certain roster transactions could move forward. Leonard is expected to return to the Toronto Raptors, although the exact transaction details remain subject to the league's procedures.

Questions about whether Kawhi is going to the Raptors have therefore become a major part of the NBA news surrounding the case. Leonard originally became a Clippers star after joining the team in 2019. He helped lead Toronto to the 2019 NBA championship before signing with Los Angeles.

The investigation has also put the spotlight on Leonard's contract and the circumstances surrounding his recruitment by the Clippers. The NBA's findings did not conclude that his playing contract itself should be canceled. Instead, the league focused on off-court compensation and related business arrangements.

Pablo Torre's reporting played an important role in bringing the issue into the public spotlight. His work raised questions about Leonard's relationship with Aspiration and other companies connected to the Clippers. The subsequent NBA investigation expanded beyond the original allegations and examined several related financial arrangements.

For Clippers supporters, the immediate concern is what happens next. The team has lost five first-round picks, faces a $30 million fine and must operate under increased league monitoring. Ballmer will also be away from official league and team activities for a full year.

The Clippers now face the challenge of maintaining their competitiveness while dealing with reduced draft capital and major organizational scrutiny. The loss of future first-round selections could affect the franchise well beyond the current NBA season.

The NBA's decision also sends a strong message to other teams about salary-cap rules. Commissioner Adam Silver said the league's collectively bargained system for determining player compensation is fundamental to the competition and that the severity of the penalties reflects the seriousness of the violations.

For now, the central facts are clear. The NBA found that the Clippers violated salary-cap circumvention rules. The franchise lost five first-round picks, received a $30 million fine, and will be monitored for five years. Steve Ballmer was suspended for one year, Lawrence Frank for six months and Gillian Zucker for one year. Leonard was fined $700,000.

The Clippers disagree with the league's conclusions and plan to challenge the sanctions. That means the legal and basketball consequences of the case could continue to develop even after the NBA's initial punishment was announced.

As the 2026-27 NBA season approaches, the Clippers will have to deal with the fallout while trying to remain competitive. The investigation involving Kawhi Leonard, Steve Ballmer, Dennis Robertson and the team's business relationships has become one of the biggest NBA stories of the year.
