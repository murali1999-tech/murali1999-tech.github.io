Amazon Plane Crash at Miami International Airport: Latest Updates

An Amazon Prime Air cargo plane crashed after overrunning a runway at Miami International Airport in Florida on September 6, 2026. The aircraft was a Boeing 767-300 freighter operated by 21 Air for Amazon's air cargo network. The aircraft was registered as N1997A and was operating Flight 7598 from San Juan, Puerto Rico, to Miami.

The accident happened shortly before 2 p.m. local time as the aircraft was landing at Miami International Airport, also known by its airport code MIA. According to information released after the crash, the Boeing 767 landed on Runway 30 but continued beyond the usable runway area. It crossed the airport perimeter and struck several vehicles before coming to rest outside the runway area and catching fire.

File photo of an Amazon Air Boeing 767 cargo aircraft. This is an illustrative image and is not the aircraft involved in the Miami crash.

At least five people were killed in the Miami plane crash and another five people were injured, with three of those injured reported to be in critical condition. Emergency crews faced a difficult situation because the aircraft crossed beyond the airport boundary and struck vehicles in the surrounding area. More than 60 Miami-Dade Fire Rescue units and about 200 personnel responded to the scene.

The pilots were trapped inside the aircraft after the crash and rescuers worked to reach them. Other victims were involved in vehicles that were struck as the cargo plane crossed the road beyond the airport. Authorities have continued working to establish the identities of the victims and determine exactly how the injuries and deaths occurred.

The aircraft involved was N1997A, a Boeing 767-33A(ER) converted freighter. The aircraft was about 32 years old and had originally operated as a passenger aircraft before being converted for cargo operations. It was being operated by 21 Air on behalf of Amazon Prime Air. Amazon does not operate every aircraft in its air cargo network directly; it works with contracted cargo airlines such as 21 Air.

Flight tracking information has provided investigators with useful information about the final portion of the flight. Data published by Flightradar24 showed that the aircraft landed at approximately 1:53 p.m. local time and was still traveling at significant speed as it reached the end of the runway. The aircraft was reported at about 112 knots when it left the usable runway area. Investigators will use flight tracking information together with aircraft recorder data and other evidence to establish what happened.

Weather conditions are also expected to be examined. Thunderstorms and gusting winds were reported around Miami International Airport at the time of the accident. However, weather alone has not been identified as the cause of the crash. Investigators will need to determine whether wind, rain, runway conditions, aircraft systems, braking performance, touchdown location, crew actions or another factor contributed to the runway overrun.

Miami International Airport experienced major disruption following the accident. A ground stop was imposed after the crash and airport runways and taxiways were temporarily closed while emergency crews responded and the area was secured. Hundreds of flights were affected through cancellations and delays. Airport operations began returning toward normal after the emergency response, although some restrictions remained while investigators and airport personnel dealt with the crash site.

The crash has also attracted attention because of the location of the aircraft after it left the airport. Miami International Airport is one of the busiest airports in the United States and is particularly important for cargo traffic. The area surrounding the airport contains roads, parking areas, warehouses and other businesses, making a runway overrun potentially dangerous beyond the airport itself.

The Federal Aviation Administration and National Transportation Safety Board are investigating the accident. Investigators are expected to examine the flight data recorder, cockpit voice recorder, aircraft maintenance records, runway information, weather conditions, air traffic control communications and the actions of the flight crew. They will also examine the condition of the aircraft's brakes, landing gear and other systems that could affect the ability of a Boeing 767 to stop after landing.

At this stage, it is important not to assume that one particular factor caused the Miami airport crash. Early videos and flight-tracking information can show what happened during the final moments, but they cannot by themselves establish the cause. The official investigation will take time and additional information may change the understanding of the accident.

The accident is also being closely watched by the aviation and cargo industries because Amazon Prime Air has become a major part of the U.S. air freight network. Cargo aircraft move packages between distribution centers and airports throughout the country, particularly overnight and on high-volume routes. A serious accident involving an aircraft operating for the network naturally raises questions about safety, although investigators have not indicated that Amazon's cargo operation itself caused this accident.
