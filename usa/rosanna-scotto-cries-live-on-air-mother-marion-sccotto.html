Rosanna Scotto Cries Live on Air While Mourning Her Mother Marion Scotto

Rosanna Scotto has been a longtime anchor of Good Day New York on FOX 5.

Rosanna Scotto became emotional and cried live on air during her return to Good Day New York on Tuesday, September 1, 2026. The longtime FOX 5 New York anchor was back at work after taking time away following the death of her mother, Marion Scotto, who died at the age of 90.

The emotional moment happened during the morning broadcast when the song "Raindrops Keep Fallin' on My Head" by B.J. Thomas played before the weather report. The familiar song unexpectedly brought back memories of Scotto's parents, and she struggled to hold back her tears while sitting on the set.

"Sorry, sorry. This song reminds me of my parents," Scotto said as she became emotional on camera. She then thanked viewers and others who had contacted her with messages and condolences following her mother's death.

Scotto also added a touch of humor during the difficult moment, saying that her mother would have been upset to see her crying on television. She then tried to compose herself and continue with the morning broadcast.

Rosanna Scotto is a longtime Good Day New York anchor and FOX 5 New York personality.

The moment came only days after Scotto announced the death of her mother. Marion Scotto died on August 26, 2026, at age 90. FOX 5 described Marion as a beloved member of the Scotto family and a familiar face to viewers who had seen her appear alongside Rosanna over the years.

Marion Scotto was much more than the mother of a television personality. She was an important figure in the Scotto family's New York restaurant business and became closely associated with Fresco by Scotto, the Manhattan restaurant that the family opened in 1993.

For many New Yorkers, Marion became a familiar and welcoming presence at the restaurant. She was also occasionally featured on FOX 5 broadcasts, particularly during holiday segments and family appearances. Her warm personality helped make her recognizable to viewers beyond the restaurant's regular customers.

Rosanna's tribute to her mother focused heavily on family. She remembered Marion as a strong woman who valued family, hard work, faith and generosity. Those themes have also been part of the way the Scotto family has described Marion's life and legacy.

Marion was married to Anthony Scotto for more than six decades. Anthony died in 2021. Rosanna has previously spoken about her parents and the importance they placed on family and their shared work.

Returning to television so soon after losing her mother was clearly difficult for Scotto. However, she later explained that going back to work was something her mother would have wanted. In a message to followers, Scotto thanked people for their support and said she was returning to work because her mother would have encouraged her to do so.

Scotto also recalled what happened after her father's death. She said Marion had encouraged the family to return to work at their restaurant very quickly. That memory helped influence Scotto's decision to return to Good Day New York rather than remain away from the program for an extended period.

The emotional broadcast quickly attracted attention online. Viewers shared messages of sympathy and support for Scotto, with many people relating to the experience of hearing a familiar song or seeing something ordinary suddenly bring back memories of someone who has died.

Scotto has been a major part of New York television for decades. FOX 5 says she joined WNYW in 1986 and has worked in several reporting and anchoring roles during her career. She eventually became one of the best-known faces of Good Day New York.

Her career has included coverage of major stories in New York and beyond. FOX 5's biography notes that she has reported from locations including Israel, Rome and across the United States. She has also received multiple Emmy Awards and other journalism honors.

Despite her long career in television, the September 1 broadcast was very personal. Instead of reporting on someone else's difficult story, Scotto was dealing with her own grief in front of the audience that has watched her for many years.

Her co-workers also offered their support during the broadcast. The atmosphere on the program reflected the fact that Scotto was returning to work while still processing the loss of her mother.

Marion Scotto's connection to Fresco by Scotto is another important part of her legacy. The restaurant became a long-running family business in Midtown Manhattan and a place where the Scotto family welcomed customers, celebrities and other prominent visitors.

Rosanna has often balanced her television career with her involvement in the family restaurant. That connection between her professional life and family life made her mother's death particularly significant for her and for viewers who had watched the family appear together on television.

The song that caused Scotto to become emotional was not part of a planned tribute. It was the unexpected timing of the music that made the moment especially powerful. A routine morning broadcast suddenly became a personal memory for the anchor.

Scotto eventually regained her composure and continued with the program. Her decision to continue working reflected the message she later shared with followers: that her mother would have wanted her to move forward.

There has also been a strong response from colleagues, friends and viewers. Messages of sympathy have come from people in television, entertainment and New York media who know Scotto and her family.

Marion Scotto's death has therefore been remembered not only as a private family loss but also as the loss of a familiar New York personality. Through Fresco by Scotto, FOX 5 appearances and her family, Marion became known to many people who had never met her personally.

Rosanna Scotto's tears on Good Day New York showed how difficult it can be to return to normal routines after losing someone close. The unexpected song brought back memories of her parents and briefly overwhelmed her during the live broadcast.

At the same time, Scotto's decision to return to work shows the way she is trying to honor her mother's wishes. She has thanked viewers for their messages and continues to navigate life without her mother while remaining part of the morning program.

For viewers who have followed Rosanna Scotto for many years, the emotional moment was a reminder that television personalities experience the same personal losses and emotions as everyone else. Her brief pause on live television became a heartfelt tribute to Marion Scotto and to the memories they shared as a family.

Rosanna Scotto remains a co-host of Good Day New York, where she continues her work at FOX 5. Her return to the program marks the beginning of a new period for her as she continues her career while mourning the loss of her mother.

The September 1 broadcast will likely remain one of the more personal moments of Scotto's long television career. A simple song brought back memories, the cameras were live, and viewers saw an unscripted expression of grief from a familiar New York television figure.
