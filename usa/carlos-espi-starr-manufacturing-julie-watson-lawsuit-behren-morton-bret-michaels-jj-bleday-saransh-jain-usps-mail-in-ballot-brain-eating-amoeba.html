Starr Manufacturing and Julie Watson Lawsuit

Starr Manufacturing Inc., a family-owned industrial equipment company based in Ohio, has filed a defamation lawsuit against Columbus resident Julie Watson over a one-star Google review, according to reports on the legal complaint.

The company filed the lawsuit on August 12 in Trumbull County Court of Common Pleas. Starr Manufacturing alleges that Watson made false and damaging statements about the company's manufacturing quality and claimed that multiple customers were dissatisfied.

The company further alleges that Watson was not a customer and that the review was connected to a personal dispute involving a relationship with a company employee. Starr Manufacturing is seeking more than $25,000 in damages, along with court costs and attorney fees.

Important: A lawsuit contains allegations and claims from the parties involved. The filing itself does not establish that the allegations are true. Watson had not publicly commented in the report available at publication.

The case highlights the increasingly complicated relationship between online reviews and defamation law. Businesses rely heavily on online ratings, while customers and members of the public also use review platforms to express opinions about companies.

USPS Mail-in Ballot Guidelines

Voters planning to return ballots by mail should pay close attention to instructions provided by their election officials. USPS publishes specific guidance for Election Mail and Ballot Mail, but individual states and local election authorities determine many of the requirements and deadlines for returning ballots.

The Postal Service's 2026 Election Mail guidance explains that election officials must indicate the proper postage required for ballot materials. Some military and overseas ballots can have special handling and postage arrangements.

For the 2026 general election, USPS has special procedures for APO/FPO/DPO absentee ballots from September 1 through November 30. Election officials must mail absentee ballots to military and overseas voters at least 45 days before the November 3 election.

Mail voting reminder: Always follow the return instructions supplied with your ballot and check your state or local election office for the applicable deadline. USPS guidance does not replace state election rules.

Brain-Eating Amoeba: Naegleria Fowleri

The phrase brain-eating amoeba refers to Naegleria fowleri, a microscopic organism that can cause a rare and usually severe brain infection called primary amebic meningoencephalitis, or PAM.

According to the CDC, Naegleria fowleri lives in warm freshwater and soil. Infection usually occurs when contaminated water enters the nose, allowing the organism to travel toward the brain. People do not become infected simply by drinking contaminated water, and the infection does not spread from person to person.

Early symptoms can resemble meningitis and may include headache, fever and a stiff neck. The illness can progress rapidly, which is why people who develop meningitis symptoms after recent exposure to warm freshwater should seek medical attention immediately.

The CDC recommends reducing exposure risk by keeping the nose closed or using a nose clip when entering warm freshwater, keeping the head above water in hot springs and using distilled or boiled water for nasal rinsing.

Health information: Naegleria fowleri infection is extremely rare. This article is for general awareness and is not a substitute for medical advice.
