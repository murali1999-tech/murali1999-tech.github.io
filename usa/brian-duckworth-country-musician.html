Brian Duckworth Dies at 56: Country Music Singer and Taylor Made Member Passes Away

Latest entertainment updates

Country music singer Brian “Nashville” Neil Duckworth has died at the age of 56. Duckworth was known for his work with the West Virginia country group Taylor Made, the family band he performed in alongside his brother Greg Duckworth and sister Wendy Williams.

According to his obituary, Duckworth died at his home in Clarksburg, West Virginia, on Monday, September 7, 2026. News of his death has brought sadness to fans of country music and to people who knew him through Taylor Made and the West Virginia music community.

Duckworth was born on December 8, 1969, in Grafton, West Virginia. Music was part of his life from an early age, particularly through the singing he shared with his siblings. His family background eventually became an important part of his professional music career.

Brian, Greg and Wendy developed their musical style through years of singing together. Their three-part family harmony became one of the defining features of Taylor Made, helping the group establish a following among country music audiences.

Before becoming known as Taylor Made, the siblings performed together as I-79. They entered the Colgate Country Showdown and discovered that they worked especially well as a group. Their family connection became an important part of their sound and identity.

Taylor Made later became known for songs including “Things You Don't Grow Out Of.” The song became one of the group's better-known recordings and helped the band gain attention in the country music industry.

The group also earned recognition from MusicRow. Taylor Made was named MusicRow's Independent Artist of the Year in 2012, a significant achievement for a family country group from West Virginia.

Taylor Made's career included opportunities to perform alongside established country artists. The group shared stages and opened shows connected with performers including Miranda Lambert, Charlie Daniels and Dierks Bentley.

For Brian Duckworth, however, music was only one part of his life. Away from performances, he worked as a dump truck driver for Wolfe's Excavating. His work earned him the affectionate nickname “Truck Boss.”

The nickname reflected his connection with the outdoors and his working life. According to his obituary, Duckworth enjoyed the job because it allowed him to spend time outside. Whether he was working or relaxing, he was happiest around nature.

That combination of music and ordinary working life made Duckworth an unusual figure in the country music world. He could perform on stage with his family and then return to a job driving a dump truck.

Duckworth was also remembered as a devoted family man. He married Tina Scheuvront Duckworth in 2010, and the couple had been together for more than 15 years before his death.

He is survived by his wife, six children and four grandchildren. His family also included a granddaughter who was on the way. His loved ones described his family relationships and memories as an important part of his life.

Duckworth was also known for his affection for his pets. His obituary remembered his animals as constant companions who brought him comfort and happiness.

His love of music remained central throughout his life. The nickname “Nashville” reflected the affection people had for his singing and his dedication to performing with Taylor Made.

The family's musical story began long before professional performances. Brian and his siblings grew up singing together, with their mother teaching them songs. Their early experiences singing around the family home helped shape the harmony that later became part of Taylor Made's identity.

For many musicians, family harmony can be difficult to reproduce outside a close group. Taylor Made's three-part sound was built naturally through years of singing together. Brian's voice was therefore not simply part of a band but part of a family tradition.

The group eventually attracted attention beyond West Virginia. Its recordings received radio exposure, and the siblings continued performing at country music events and festivals.

“Things You Don't Grow Out Of” became an important song for Taylor Made. The band's music was rooted in traditional country influences while also reflecting the personality and experiences of the siblings.

Brian's death means that fans are now looking back at the group's recordings and performances as part of his musical legacy. His work with Taylor Made remains one of the clearest ways audiences can remember his contribution to country music.

The news of Duckworth's death has also generated interest in his cause of death. At this time, the published obituary does not disclose a cause of death. It is important not to repeat unverified claims about the reason for his passing.

What has been confirmed is that Duckworth died at his home in Clarksburg on September 7, 2026, at the age of 56. The available obituary provides details about his family, career, music and funeral arrangements but does not state a cause.
