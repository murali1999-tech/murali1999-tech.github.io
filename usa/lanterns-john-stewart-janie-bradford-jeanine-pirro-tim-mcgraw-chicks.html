Motown Legend Janie Bradford Passes Away

The music world is remembering Janie Bradford Hobbs, an influential early member of Motown who died at the age of 87. Bradford was closely connected with the early development of the legendary Detroit record company and became an important songwriter and publishing executive.

Bradford joined Motown in its early years, initially working as a receptionist. Her career quickly developed beyond administrative duties, and she became involved in songwriting and the company's growing music business. She co-wrote “Money (That's What I Want)” with Motown founder Berry Gordy. The song, recorded by Barrett Strong, became one of the foundational hits associated with the early Motown story.

Bradford also co-wrote “Too Busy Thinking About My Baby,” a song that became closely associated with Marvin Gaye after earlier recordings by The Temptations. Her work connected her with some of the most important artists of the Motown era, including Stevie Wonder, The Supremes, Martha & The Vandellas and other legendary performers.

Beyond songwriting, Bradford played an important role in Motown's publishing operation. She later worked with Jobete, Motown's publishing arm, and became involved in recognizing and supporting music-industry veterans. Her career demonstrated that Motown's success depended not only on its famous singers but also on the writers, publishers, executives and behind-the-scenes figures who helped build the company.

Her death has renewed interest in the early history of Motown and the people who helped establish the label as a major force in popular music. Bradford's contribution remains connected to songs that continued to be recorded and performed by generations of musicians.

Jeanine Pirro Remains in the Spotlight

Jeanine Pirro continues to attract attention in Washington as the U.S. attorney for the District of Columbia. The former judge and television personality has remained a prominent public figure because of her law-enforcement role and her close association with President Donald Trump.

Pirro has faced scrutiny following disputes surrounding cases involving alleged vandalism and damage at the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool. Her office dropped charges against several individuals after determining that the damage in one prominent case was connected to structural or construction-related problems rather than the alleged conduct originally suspected.

The decision drew criticism from Trump, creating an unusual public disagreement between the president and a senior federal prosecutor appointed during his administration. The controversy has fueled questions about Pirro's future, although she has continued performing her duties as Washington's U.S. attorney.

Pirro has also continued to speak publicly about crime, law enforcement and public safety in Washington. Her background in television and criminal justice has made her one of the more recognizable federal prosecutors in the country.
