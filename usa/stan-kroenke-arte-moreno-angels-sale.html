Stan Kroenke Agrees to Buy Los Angeles Angels From Arte Moreno

Updated September 2, 2026

Stan Kroenke is set to become the new controlling owner of the Los Angeles Angels after Kroenke Sports & Entertainment reached a definitive agreement to acquire a controlling interest in the Major League Baseball franchise from the Moreno family. The announcement on September 1, 2026, represents a major change for Angels baseball and brings one of the biggest sports ownership groups in the United States into Major League Baseball.

The transaction is expected to close during the first quarter of 2027, subject to approval from Major League Baseball and other customary closing conditions. The Angels and Kroenke Sports & Entertainment announced the agreement, while the exact purchase price was not included in the official announcement.

Multiple reports have put the value of the transaction at about $4 billion. If completed at that level, the Angels sale would establish a new record for the highest price paid for an MLB franchise. The reported figure is above the $3.9 billion sale of the San Diego Padres earlier in 2026.

The deal ends Arte Moreno's ownership of the Angels after more than two decades. Moreno purchased the franchise in 2003, shortly after the organization won its first and only World Series championship in 2002.

During Moreno's ownership, the team produced several memorable individual performances, most notably from Mike Trout and Shohei Ohtani. However, the franchise struggled to maintain consistent postseason success. The Angels have not returned to the playoffs since 2014, creating one of the longest postseason droughts in Major League Baseball.

Moreno had previously explored selling the Angels. In August 2022, he announced that the franchise would explore a potential sale. Several months later, he ended that process and decided to retain ownership. Four years later, the Angels are officially changing hands.

For fans in Anaheim and Southern California, the sale could mark the beginning of a new period for the franchise. The Angels have dealt with criticism over their performance, stadium situation and long-term direction, while fans have repeatedly called for changes at the ownership level.

Stan Kroenke already has one of the largest sports portfolios in the world. Through Kroenke Sports & Entertainment, he controls the Los Angeles Rams of the NFL, the Denver Nuggets of the NBA, the Colorado Avalanche of the NHL, the Colorado Rapids of Major League Soccer and Arsenal of the English Premier League.

Kroenke's ownership of the Rams makes the Angels transaction particularly significant for the Los Angeles sports market. The Rams returned to Los Angeles under Kroenke's ownership in 2016 and later won Super Bowl LVI at SoFi Stadium.

Kroenke also owns the Denver Nuggets, who won the NBA championship in 2023, and the Colorado Avalanche, who won the Stanley Cup in 2022. His sports portfolio therefore includes teams competing in several of the largest professional leagues in North America and Europe.

The Angels will be the latest major addition to that portfolio and Kroenke's first MLB franchise. His company will have teams in the NFL, MLB, NBA, NHL, MLS and English Premier League once the Angels acquisition is completed.

Kroenke's personal wealth is another reason the transaction has attracted attention. Forbes estimated Stan Kroenke's real-time net worth at approximately $24.3 billion as of September 1, 2026. His wealth comes from a combination of sports ownership, real estate and other business investments.

Kroenke's real estate business has been a major part of his fortune. Forbes has reported that his holdings include roughly 60 million square feet of real estate, much of it involving shopping centers. He also has extensive ranching and land interests in the United States and Canada.

The reported $4 billion Angels valuation is therefore a significant transaction even for a billionaire sports owner with an extensive portfolio. The purchase will add baseball to a sports empire that already stretches across several major professional competitions.

The future of the Angels' name and stadium situation will also be watched closely. The franchise currently plays at Angel Stadium in Anaheim, where it has played since 1966. The team's identity has changed over the years, including periods when it was known as the California Angels, Anaheim Angels and Los Angeles Angels.

The stadium lease is another important issue for the incoming ownership group. The existing arrangement runs into the early 2030s, meaning Kroenke and Anaheim officials will have to consider the long-term future of the ballpark and surrounding property.

There has also been renewed discussion about the Angels' relationship with Anaheim. The franchise currently uses the Los Angeles Angels name even though Angel Stadium is located in Anaheim. A future ownership decision could potentially bring renewed attention to the Anaheim Angels name, although no official name change has been announced.

The sale does not immediately change the day-to-day ownership of the club. Until the transaction receives the required MLB approvals and closes, Arte Moreno remains the controlling owner. The expected first-quarter 2027 closing date means the transition will take place after the 2026 season.

Major League Baseball approval is an important step in every ownership transaction. MLB owners must approve the transfer before a new controlling owner can officially take over a franchise.

The Angels enter this ownership transition after another difficult season. The team has struggled to compete in the American League West and is on course for another losing season. The franchise has also gone through changes in its baseball operations department, including changes involving its general manager and front office.

The arrival of Kroenke could therefore bring questions about whether the new ownership group will make significant investments in baseball operations. Fans will be watching to see whether the organization changes its approach to roster construction, player development, scouting and free-agent spending.

One of the biggest challenges facing the Angels is turning a valuable Southern California market into sustained success on the field. The franchise has had major stars but has struggled to build a consistently competitive roster around them.

The Angels have also benefited from some of the biggest names in modern baseball. Mike Trout became one of the game's defining players during his time with the organization, while Shohei Ohtani established himself as a unique two-way superstar before leaving the team for the Los Angeles Dodgers.

The new ownership era will be judged primarily by what happens next. A large financial commitment alone does not guarantee success in baseball. The organization will need strong front-office leadership, effective player development and smart roster decisions if it wants to end its lengthy playoff drought.

For Stan Kroenke, the Angels acquisition represents another expansion of an already enormous sports empire. For Arte Moreno, it closes a 23-year chapter as the team's owner. For Angels fans, the sale offers the possibility of a fresh start after more than a decade without postseason baseball.

The transaction remains subject to MLB approval, so the ownership change is not technically complete yet. If all conditions are satisfied, Kroenke Sports & Entertainment is expected to take control of the Angels in the first quarter of 2027.

The coming months will likely bring more information about Kroenke's plans, the future of Angel Stadium, the team's baseball operations structure and the long-term direction of the franchise. Until then, the confirmed development is that Kroenke Sports & Entertainment has reached a definitive agreement to acquire a controlling interest in the Los Angeles Angels from the Moreno family.
