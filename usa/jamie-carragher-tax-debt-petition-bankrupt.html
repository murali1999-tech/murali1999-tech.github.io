Jamie Carragher Faces Bankruptcy Petition Over Unpaid Tax Bill

Jamie Carragher is facing a bankruptcy petition from HM Revenue and Customs over an unpaid tax bill, according to court filings reported on Thursday. The former Liverpool and England defender has not been declared bankrupt, but HMRC has taken legal action that could potentially lead to a bankruptcy order if the matter is not resolved.

The petition was filed at the High Court in London on Wednesday against James Lee Duncan Carragher, the former footballer's full legal name. The news has attracted considerable attention because Carragher remains one of the most recognizable former Liverpool players and has built a successful career in television and sports broadcasting since retiring from football.

Carragher's representatives have described the situation as a private tax matter. A spokesperson said the issue should have been resolved sooner and that appropriate steps were being taken to settle it. They also indicated that the matter is expected to be resolved imminently and will not proceed further.

HMRC did not comment on Carragher's individual case. The tax authority said generally that it takes a supportive approach toward people with tax debts and can offer options such as instalment plans. HMRC also said that it only seeks bankruptcy as a last resort.

The amount of Carragher's alleged unpaid tax has not been publicly disclosed. That means claims circulating online about the size of the debt should be treated carefully until reliable documentation or an official statement provides more information.

It is also important to distinguish between facing a bankruptcy petition and actually being bankrupt. A petition is a legal request for a court to consider bankruptcy. The court can ultimately dismiss, delay or stop a petition, or make a bankruptcy order depending on the circumstances of the case.

Carragher, 48, spent his entire professional playing career with Liverpool. He made 737 appearances for the club between 1996 and 2013 and won major trophies including the Champions League, FA Cup, League Cup and UEFA Cup.

Since retiring, Carragher has become a familiar face on Sky Sports and regularly appears on Premier League coverage. He also works with CBS Sports on Champions League coverage and is a regular contributor to the Stick to Football podcast alongside former players and broadcasters including Gary Neville.
