DWP Pension Credit Review: Why Bank Statements May Be Requested

The Department for Work and Pensions can review Pension Credit claims to make sure people continue to receive the correct amount of support. As part of a review, some claimants may be asked to provide information about their income, savings and financial circumstances. Bank statements can sometimes be requested when the DWP needs evidence to check a person's circumstances.

Pension Credit is a means-tested benefit for people who have reached State Pension age and have a relatively low income. It is separate from the State Pension and is designed to provide additional financial support. Because eligibility depends on circumstances such as income and savings, the DWP may need to check whether the information used to calculate an award remains accurate.

A review does not automatically mean that a claimant has done anything wrong. Benefit reviews are used to check that payments are correct. The DWP may contact someone by letter or another official method and explain what information is needed and how it should be provided.

If bank statements are requested, the DWP may use them to verify information about money coming into an account or financial resources that could affect entitlement. Claimants should provide the information requested within the deadline given by the department. If someone is unsure about a request, they should contact the DWP using an official phone number or website rather than responding to suspicious messages.

Savings and capital can be relevant to Pension Credit calculations. However, having money in a bank account does not automatically mean that someone will lose Pension Credit. The rules are more complicated than simply looking at a bank balance, and different types of income and capital can be treated differently.

People should also remember that ordinary payments appearing in a bank account do not necessarily represent earnings. A bank statement can contain many different transactions, including transfers between personal accounts, refunds and other payments. The DWP may ask questions if it needs clarification about a particular transaction.

Keeping financial records organized can make a review easier. Claimants may want to keep copies of letters, documents and information they send to the DWP. If circumstances have changed, such as a change in income, savings, living arrangements or other relevant details, the DWP should be informed when required.

There are also circumstances in which a person may need help responding to a review. Citizens Advice and other recognized welfare rights organizations can provide guidance about benefit entitlement and how to respond to requests for information.

Anyone receiving a Pension Credit review letter should read it carefully and check exactly what documents are being requested. It is important not to ignore the correspondence because failing to provide information when required could lead to a delay or change in payments.

The key point is that a request for bank statements during a DWP Pension Credit review is not, by itself, proof of wrongdoing. It can simply be part of the department's process for checking that a claimant's financial circumstances and benefit award remain correct.
