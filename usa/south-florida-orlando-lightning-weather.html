South Florida and Orlando Weather: Lightning and Thunderstorms Expected

South Florida and Orlando are dealing with another hot, humid and stormy stretch of weather as late August continues. Afternoon thunderstorms are expected across much of Florida, with lightning, heavy rainfall and gusty winds becoming the main concerns for people outdoors.

In Orlando, Thursday is expected to bring a high near 92 degrees Fahrenheit, with thunderstorms developing during the afternoon. The National Weather Service says scattered to numerous storms are expected across east central Florida, with rain chances around 50 to 70 percent. Frequent lightning, strong wind gusts and locally heavy rainfall are possible.

The weather pattern is typical for this time of year, but individual storms can still become dangerous very quickly. Strong thunderstorms may produce wind gusts of 45 to 55 mph, while heavy downpours could cause temporary flooding in urban locations and areas with poor drainage.

Lightning is one of the biggest concerns around Orlando. Storms can produce frequent cloud-to-ground strikes, and lightning can travel well away from the main area of heavy rain. Anyone who hears thunder should move indoors rather than waiting for the storm to arrive directly overhead.

South Florida is also expecting another active afternoon. The National Weather Service office in Miami says scattered showers and thunderstorms are likely, with storms developing along sea-breeze boundaries and moving inland. Heavy rainfall, frequent lightning and gusty winds are possible with stronger storms.

Temperatures across South Florida are expected to remain in the low to mid 90s during the afternoon. The combination of high temperatures and humidity can make conditions feel considerably hotter, especially for people working or exercising outside.

Orlando's storm activity can increase during the afternoon as the Atlantic sea breeze moves inland. Storms can also interact with other boundaries near central Florida, producing periods of intense rain and lightning. Travel conditions can change quickly when one of these storms moves across the area.

The forecast also suggests that the unsettled pattern will continue beyond Thursday. More thunderstorms are expected Friday and through the weekend, with hot and humid conditions remaining in place. The exact location and timing of storms can change, so residents should check updated local forecasts before making outdoor plans.

For drivers, heavy rain can reduce visibility and create standing water on roads. People attending outdoor events, visiting theme parks or spending time near pools should pay particular attention to lightning alerts. The safest response to thunder is to seek shelter inside a substantial building or enclosed vehicle and wait until the storm has passed.

Florida's summer storm season can bring several rounds of thunderstorms in a single afternoon. Even when the morning begins with sunshine, conditions can change rapidly after temperatures rise. For Orlando and South Florida, the message for the next several days is fairly simple: expect heat, humidity, thunderstorms and frequent lightning, particularly during the afternoon and evening.
