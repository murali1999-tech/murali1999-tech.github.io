Starman Optical and GoPro: What the GPRO Merger Means

GoPro has announced a major change to its business with a definitive agreement to merge with Starman Optical, a privately held optical-photonics company. The announcement was made on September 1, 2026, and immediately put GoPro and its Nasdaq ticker GPRO in the spotlight. The transaction is designed to recapitalize GoPro, strengthen its balance sheet and move the company into additional markets connected to optical technology, artificial intelligence infrastructure, government, defense and aerospace.

Under the agreement, GoPro shareholders are expected to receive an aggregate cash payment of $285 million, equivalent to approximately $1.14 per share. The final amount can be adjusted based on GoPro's net working capital at the time the transaction closes. Existing GoPro shareholders are also expected to retain approximately 10 percent ownership of the outstanding shares of the combined company.

GoPro camera technology shown for illustration.

The deal is significant because Starman Optical operates in a very different part of the technology market. Starman is focused on optical-photonics technology, including optical transceivers and related components used for high-speed data transmission. Its technology is aimed at markets where large amounts of data need to move quickly between computing systems.

The agreement says Starman's U.S.-made optical transceivers are expected to become part of GoPro's portfolio. The companies believe the combination can give GoPro a presence in the growing optical-transceiver market, particularly as demand for data-center infrastructure increases with the expansion of artificial intelligence.

Artificial intelligence systems require enormous amounts of computing power and data movement. Modern AI data centers depend on high-speed networking equipment to move information between servers and other components. Optical technology is an important part of that infrastructure because fiber-optic communication can carry large amounts of information over high speeds and distances.

GoPro therefore sees an opportunity to use its existing expertise in optics and imaging while adding Starman's optical-transceiver business. The company has spent more than two decades developing imaging products and has built a substantial intellectual-property portfolio. GoPro says its portfolio includes more than 2,500 U.S. patents.

The merger is also intended to improve GoPro's financial position. GoPro currently has approximately $92 million of outstanding debt, and the announcement says that debt is expected to be repaid in full when the transaction closes. That would leave the combined company with a substantially debt-free balance sheet.

Importantly for investors following GPRO, GoPro is not being taken completely private under the announced transaction. The company says it will remain publicly listed on Nasdaq. Existing consumer products and the GoPro subscription and cloud platform will also continue to be supported.

That means GoPro's familiar consumer business is not simply disappearing. The company plans to continue investing in cameras and related products while expanding into commercial and strategic markets. The broader strategy includes applications involving defense, government, robotics and aerospace.

The companies also expect the merger to increase domestic manufacturing of optical components. Starman's business includes U.S.-made optical transceivers, and the combined company intends to use that manufacturing platform as part of a wider effort to develop critical optical and imaging technology in the United States.

GoPro founder and CEO Nicholas Woodman said the combination is expected to help the company expand across consumer, commercial and defense markets. The company sees an opportunity to combine its imaging and optical expertise with Starman's photonics technology and manufacturing capabilities.

The transaction has already been approved by the boards of GoPro and Starman Optical. However, the merger has not yet closed. The companies expect the transaction to close by the end of 2026, subject to regulatory approvals, approval by GoPro stockholders and other customary closing conditions.

GoPro shareholders will therefore have an important role in the process. The company plans to provide additional information through its filings and shareholder materials. Investors are expected to receive more details about the transaction, including the formal terms and information needed before any shareholder vote.

The announcement also comes at an important time for GoPro. The company has been working to expand beyond its traditional action-camera business. While GoPro remains widely recognized for rugged cameras used by athletes, travelers and creators, the company has also been developing products aimed at professional and commercial applications.

The proposed combination with Starman represents a much broader technology strategy. Instead of relying only on consumer camera sales, GoPro would have an additional business connected to the infrastructure supporting AI and high-speed data networks. The company would also have opportunities to apply imaging and optical technologies in government, defense, robotics and aerospace.

For people searching for Starman Optical, it is important to understand that Starman is a privately held company rather than a separately traded Nasdaq stock. GoPro is the publicly traded company involved in the transaction, and GPRO is its Nasdaq ticker symbol.

The announced $285 million transaction value is another important number. GoPro shareholders are expected to receive approximately $1.14 per share in cash, while retaining about 10 percent ownership of the combined company. The agreement also provides for repayment of GoPro's approximately $92 million in debt when the transaction closes.

The final outcome will depend on the required approvals and closing conditions. Until the transaction is completed, GoPro remains an independent publicly listed company and continues operating its existing consumer business. The expected year-end 2026 closing date means there will likely be additional announcements and regulatory filings before the deal is finalized.

For GPRO investors, the merger creates both a significant change in strategy and a new potential growth direction. GoPro will retain its camera and subscription businesses while gaining exposure to optical-photonics technology and AI infrastructure. The success of the strategy will ultimately depend on execution, demand for optical transceivers and the company's ability to combine the two businesses effectively.

As of September 1, 2026, the key facts are clear. GoPro and Starman Optical have signed a definitive merger agreement. The announced transaction is valued at $285 million, or approximately $1.14 per GoPro share, subject to potential adjustment. GoPro shareholders are expected to retain approximately 10 percent of the combined company, GoPro's approximately $92 million debt is expected to be repaid, and the company is expected to remain listed on Nasdaq.

The proposed merger marks one of the biggest strategic changes in GoPro's history. By combining consumer imaging with optical-photonics technology, the company is attempting to position itself for opportunities in AI data centers, commercial technology, defense, government, robotics and aerospace while continuing to support the GoPro products already used by consumers around the world.
