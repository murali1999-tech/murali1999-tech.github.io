Central Indiana Floods

Central Indiana is dealing with severe flooding following exceptionally heavy rainfall and powerful storms. The flooding has affected Indianapolis and surrounding communities, with emergency crews carrying out rescues and evacuations.

According to reports from the Associated Press, at least five people had died in Indiana following the severe weather and flooding, while more than 11 inches of rain fell in some locations over two days. The White River reached record levels in parts of central Indiana.

Reuters subsequently reported that Indiana emergency officials said at least six people had died, including a four-year-old boy, following the storms and flooding.

Flood safety: Never drive or walk through moving floodwater. Follow evacuation instructions from local emergency authorities and avoid flooded roads and bridges.

Indianapolis Flooding

Indianapolis has been among the communities affected by the flooding. Emergency officials have conducted rescues as water levels rose along the White River and nearby waterways.

The Associated Press reported that Indianapolis experienced its worst flooding in more than 30 years, while emergency crews rescued dozens of people and pets. Several counties also declared local disasters.

The White River reportedly rose beyond 24 feet in communities including Anderson and Noblesville, exceeding historical records set in 1913. Flash-flood warnings remained active across portions of central Indiana as authorities continued monitoring river levels.

Indiana Flooding: What Happened?

The flooding followed a period of intense rainfall across Indiana. The combination of saturated ground, rapidly rising rivers and additional storms created dangerous conditions across central parts of the state.

Residents in vulnerable areas were urged to monitor emergency alerts and prepare for possible evacuation. The flooding has also caused road closures, power disruptions and significant property damage in affected communities.
