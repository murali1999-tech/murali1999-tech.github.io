Manchester Airport Data Breach: Cyber Attack Affects Customer Information

Manchester Airport has been caught up in a major cybersecurity incident after Manchester Airports Group confirmed that an unauthorised third party accessed customer information. The group operates Manchester Airport, London Stansted Airport and East Midlands Airport, and said the incident affected data connected with services at all three airports.

Manchester Airports Group said information connected to car park, lounge and Fast Track bookings, as well as airport Wi-Fi registrations, was obtained. The company said it immediately contained the incident and began working with specialist cybersecurity advisers and relevant authorities.

Reports indicate that information belonging to about 8.7 million customers was accessed. The information includes email addresses, phone numbers, vehicle registration numbers and postcodes. Most of the affected information is understood to be connected with airport Wi-Fi registrations and customer bookings.

One important point for customers is that the affected system did not hold bank or payment details. Manchester Airports Group has said that customers' banking and payment information was not compromised by the incident.

The cyber attack has not caused disruption to airport operations. Manchester Airport continues to operate, and passengers can still travel through the airport. Customer parking services are also continuing to operate normally, according to Manchester Airports Group.

London Stansted Airport has also been affected because it is part of Manchester Airports Group. Customers who have used Stansted services should be aware that their information may be among the data accessed during the incident.

The incident is particularly relevant to people who have recently booked Manchester Airport parking. Vehicle registration numbers, postcodes and contact information associated with parking bookings may have been accessed. Customers should be cautious if they receive unexpected messages referring to airport parking payments, refunds or bookings.

Customers should also watch out for phishing emails, text messages and phone calls. A criminal who has obtained an email address, phone number or booking-related information could potentially use those details to make a fraudulent message appear genuine. Customers should avoid clicking suspicious links or providing banking information in response to unexpected communications.

The breach does not appear to have compromised aviation security or passenger safety. Manchester Airports Group has specifically stated that passenger safety and aviation security were not affected and that airport operations remain unaffected.

The investigation is continuing, and authorities have been informed. More information may become available as cybersecurity specialists examine how the unauthorised party obtained access and determine exactly which customer records were affected.

For passengers, the main issue at present is protecting personal information rather than changing travel plans. Manchester Airport and Stansted Airport remain operational, while parking and other customer services continue. Anyone who receives an unexpected message claiming to be from an airport should verify it through an official airport website rather than responding directly.
