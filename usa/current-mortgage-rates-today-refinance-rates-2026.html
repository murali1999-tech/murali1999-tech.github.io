Mortgage interest rates are one of the most important factors to consider when purchasing a home or refinancing an existing mortgage. Even a small difference in the interest rate can significantly change the total amount of interest paid over the life of a loan. Mortgage rates today are influenced by several factors, including inflation, economic growth, Federal Reserve policy expectations, bond-market conditions and lender competition.

What Are Mortgage Rates Today?

Current mortgage rates refer to the interest rates lenders are offering borrowers for home loans at a particular time. The rate you actually receive may be different from an advertised average because lenders consider credit score, loan amount, down payment, property type, debt-to-income ratio, occupancy and other factors.

A 30-year fixed-rate mortgage is one of the most commonly used home loans in the United States. It generally provides predictable monthly principal and interest payments for the entire loan term. A 15-year mortgage usually has a higher monthly payment but can allow borrowers to pay off the loan sooner and potentially pay substantially less interest over time.

Mortgage Refinance Rates

Mortgage refinance rates are rates offered to homeowners who replace an existing mortgage with a new loan. Homeowners may refinance to obtain a lower interest rate, reduce their monthly payment, change the loan term, switch between loan types or access available home equity.

Refinancing does not automatically save money. Borrowers should compare the new interest rate with their existing rate and consider closing costs, lender fees, appraisal expenses and the remaining time on their current mortgage. A lower rate can still result in limited savings if refinancing costs are high or the homeowner plans to move soon.

What Causes Mortgage Rates to Change?

Mortgage rates can move in response to economic data and financial-market expectations. Inflation reports, employment figures, economic growth, Federal Reserve decisions and movements in U.S. Treasury yields can all influence mortgage pricing.

It is important to remember that the Federal Reserve does not directly set the rate for a typical 30-year fixed mortgage. Instead, mortgage rates are strongly influenced by the broader bond market and investor expectations. Lenders then adjust their pricing according to market conditions, operating costs and borrower risk.

30-Year vs. 15-Year Mortgage

A 30-year mortgage generally offers a lower monthly payment because the loan is spread over a longer period. However, making payments for three decades can result in substantially more total interest.

A 15-year mortgage usually requires a larger monthly payment but allows borrowers to build equity faster and repay the debt sooner. The best choice depends on income, financial goals, emergency savings and the borrower's expected time in the property.

How to Get a Better Mortgage Rate

Borrowers can improve their chances of receiving competitive mortgage pricing by maintaining a strong credit profile, reducing outstanding debts, comparing multiple lenders and considering an appropriate down payment. Shopping around can be particularly important because lenders may offer different rates and fees to borrowers with similar financial profiles.

Before accepting a mortgage offer, compare both the interest rate and the annual percentage rate. The APR can provide additional information because it incorporates certain loan costs beyond the stated interest rate. Borrowers should also review points, lender credits, closing costs and other fees.

About This Mortgage Rate Page

This page is intended to provide a convenient starting point for people researching current mortgage rates, mortgage refinance rates and mortgage interest rates. Mortgage pricing can change rapidly, so visitors should treat displayed market information as a snapshot rather than a guaranteed offer.

When comparing mortgage offers, look beyond the headline rate. Consider the loan term, APR, points, origination charges, closing costs, prepayment provisions and estimated monthly payment. A mortgage with a slightly lower advertised rate may not always be the least expensive option once all costs are considered.

For refinancing, calculate the break-even period by comparing total refinancing costs with expected monthly savings. For example, if refinancing costs $6,000 and the new mortgage saves $300 per month, the approximate break-even period would be 20 months, before considering taxes, changes in loan term and other costs.

Mortgage rates today can differ from one lender to another, and rates available to an individual borrower depend on personal financial circumstances. Always obtain personalized quotes and review official loan estimates before proceeding.
