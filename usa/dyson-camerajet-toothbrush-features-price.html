Dyson CameraJet Toothbrush: Price, Features and How It Works

Dyson has entered the electric toothbrush market with a product that takes a very different approach to everyday oral care. The new Dyson CameraJet is designed to do more than simply move bristles across the teeth. It combines an electric toothbrush, an integrated camera, artificial intelligence, targeted liquid flossing and smartphone connectivity in one device.

The Dyson CameraJet was unveiled in Paris on September 1, 2026. Dyson says the product is the result of six years of research, development and engineering. The company has built a small camera directly into the brush head so the device can see areas between the teeth while the user is brushing. It can then identify gaps and direct a small jet of mouth rinse toward those areas.

Dyson CameraJet electric toothbrush. Image: Dyson.

One of the most interesting parts of the Dyson toothbrush is its camera system. The CameraJet uses a 100,000-pixel macro lens camera and Dyson's Gap Optical Targeting technology. According to Dyson, the camera analyzes 28 images every second. Machine-learning technology processes the information and looks for gaps between teeth that need additional cleaning.

When the system detects an area that needs attention, the toothbrush can activate a conical jet of mouth rinse. The liquid is directed between the teeth in an attempt to clean places that normal brushing can miss. Dyson says the system is designed to precision-floss while the user brushes, making the product a combination of brushing and interdental cleaning.

The CameraJet also offers different ways to use the device. Users can allow the system to automatically detect gaps and activate the jet, or they can use manual controls. There are also three brushing intensity options: Gentle, Variable and Deep clean. This gives users more control over how the toothbrush feels during everyday use.

The brush itself uses a contoured dual-bristle head. Dyson says the design is intended to clean along the gumline while also helping to remove surface stains. The brush head contains an RFID system that monitors wear and can indicate when replacement is needed. Dyson has also designed the brush head specifically for the CameraJet system.

Electric toothbrush and oral-care equipment shown for general illustration.

Another important feature is the toothbrush's variable sonic movement. Dyson says its motor keeps the bristles moving continuously and can oscillate at 245 times per second. The company has designed the system to reduce stalling of the bristles during brushing, particularly in areas that can be difficult to reach.

The CameraJet connects to the MyDyson app using Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. The app can provide live cleaning footage from the integrated camera, allowing users to see parts of their mouth while they brush. It can also provide guided cleaning, coverage mapping, technique feedback and information about brushing sessions.

Privacy is another part of the product's design. Dyson says images from the camera are processed on the device and are not stored or shared. The company's product information says the camera images are deleted from the app immediately after viewing. App features can vary depending on the market and require a compatible mobile device and internet connection.

The toothbrush is also designed around a dedicated refill and charging system. Its anti-gravity tank is intended to reduce air pockets and allow the liquid jet to work at different angles. Dyson says the targeted jet can deliver up to 0.15 millilitres of liquid between the teeth, while the diaphragm pump can release the jet very quickly.

The Dyson CameraJet is priced at $499.99 in the United States. At launch, it is available in Ceramic Ultra Blue and Ceramic Pink. The package includes the toothbrush, a refill dock, two regular brush heads and a charging cable. Dyson also says the device is waterproof and can provide up to 14 complete cleans on a single charge.

Dyson is also developing a wider dental system around the toothbrush. The company has introduced non-foaming toothpaste and low-foaming mouth rinse designed to work with the camera system. The reason for the low-foam formulas is straightforward: excessive foam could interfere with the camera's ability to see the spaces between teeth.

The company says its toothpaste and mouth rinse are designed to optimize the performance of the CameraJet. Several flavor options are planned for the dental products, including peppermint, spearmint and ginger mint. These products are intended to complement the toothbrush rather than being ordinary accessories.

Dyson has also published testing information for the CameraJet. The company says a clinical test involving 71 participants found improved gum health after four weeks of twice-daily use with Dyson formulations compared with a manual toothbrush. Dyson also publishes laboratory comparisons involving plaque removal. These are company-reported results, so independent testing and longer-term reviews will be useful as the product becomes more widely available.

The launch makes the Dyson toothbrush one of the most unusual premium electric toothbrushes currently available. Traditional electric toothbrushes concentrate mainly on brushing motion, pressure sensing and timers. Dyson is attempting to add visual information and targeted liquid cleaning to the process. The goal is to help users see where they are brushing and give additional attention to spaces between the teeth.

There is also a practical question about whether users need all of this technology. The CameraJet is considerably more expensive than ordinary electric toothbrushes, and users still need to maintain regular brushing habits and other recommended oral-care practices. Technology can provide feedback and convenience, but it does not replace professional dental care or good daily habits.

For people interested in smart home and personal-care technology, however, the Dyson CameraJet is an important new product. Its camera, machine-learning system, targeted liquid jet, app connectivity and replaceable battery make it considerably more advanced than a basic electric toothbrush.

For now, the biggest point of interest is the CameraJet's ability to combine brushing and targeted flossing. Dyson has designed the system around a simple idea: identify the spaces that are difficult to see and direct cleaning action exactly where it is needed. Whether that approach becomes a new standard in electric toothbrushes will depend on independent reviews, long-term reliability and how users respond to the premium price.

The Dyson CameraJet is therefore more than a conventional electric toothbrush. It is Dyson's attempt to bring cameras, artificial intelligence and connected technology into everyday dental care. With its September 1, 2026 launch, it marks a new chapter for Dyson and gives consumers another high-tech option for maintaining their daily oral-care routine.
