Elizabeth Holmes and You Can See Everything: Nathan Fielder's A24 Documentary

Elizabeth Holmes is back in the spotlight with a surprising new documentary called You Can See Everything. The film comes from A24 and is directed by Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim. The project had been kept remarkably quiet before its surprise announcement at the 2026 Telluride Film Festival, where it was presented as a secret screening. The documentary has quickly become one of the most talked-about new movie projects because it combines the story of the Theranos founder with the unusual filmmaking style of Nathan Fielder.

You Can See Everything is not simply another retelling of the Theranos scandal. The documentary follows Holmes herself and begins 34 days before she was sent to prison. According to the film's official description, Holmes invited a skeptical film crew to document her every move. What starts as an intimate portrait eventually develops into a three-year journey involving Holmes, the filmmakers and the complicated questions surrounding her public image and past.

For anyone asking who Elizabeth Holmes is, she is the founder of Theranos, the Silicon Valley health technology company that promised to revolutionize blood testing. Holmes became one of the most famous young entrepreneurs in America after claiming that her company's technology could perform many medical tests using only a small amount of blood. Theranos attracted enormous investment and attention before serious questions were raised about whether the technology worked as advertised.

The collapse of Theranos became one of the biggest business scandals of the modern technology era. Investigations and reporting raised questions about the company's testing technology and claims. Holmes was eventually prosecuted and convicted of defrauding investors. She began serving her prison sentence in 2023. Her story has already been examined in documentaries, books, podcasts and television productions, but You Can See Everything offers something very different because the filmmakers were able to spend time with Holmes herself.

Nathan Fielder's involvement is one of the biggest reasons the documentary has attracted so much attention. Fielder is known for Nathan for You, The Rehearsal and The Curse. His work often plays with the boundary between reality and performance. He creates unusual situations involving real people and then explores how those people react when normal social rules are changed.

That approach makes Fielder an especially interesting filmmaker to place behind the camera with Elizabeth Holmes. Holmes spent years developing a carefully controlled public image as the visionary founder of Theranos. Fielder's work, on the other hand, often examines what happens when carefully planned situations become unpredictable. The meeting between those two worlds could make You Can See Everything very different from a conventional celebrity or business documentary.

Lance Oppenheim is co-directing the movie with Fielder. Oppenheim is an experienced documentary filmmaker whose previous work includes Some Kind of Heaven, Spermworld and the documentary series Ren Faire. His style has a cinematic quality, while Fielder brings a more experimental and sometimes uncomfortable approach to real-life subjects. Their collaboration gives the movie an unusual combination of traditional documentary filmmaking and Fielder's distinctive sense of humor and observation.

The first teaser for You Can See Everything immediately shows why the project is attracting attention. Fielder questions Holmes about whether she is being genuine. Holmes insists that she is being real and questions why she would deceive the filmmakers. The conversation is uncomfortable because viewers already know the history surrounding Theranos. Instead of simply explaining the scandal, the teaser places Holmes in a direct conversation with a filmmaker who appears determined to challenge what she says.

The timing of the documentary is particularly important. Holmes entered prison in May 2023, and the movie's story begins shortly before that happened. This gives the filmmakers access to a period in Holmes's life when she was facing the consequences of the Theranos scandal and preparing to begin her sentence. Instead of looking at Holmes entirely through old interviews and archival footage, the documentary observes her at a completely different point in her life.

The title You Can See Everything is also an intriguing choice. Holmes became known for controlling how she presented herself to investors, journalists and the public. The documentary appears to explore the difference between what a person says about themselves and what an audience actually sees. With cameras following Holmes for an extended period, the filmmakers have an opportunity to examine moments that would not normally appear in a traditional interview-based documentary.

A24 has become known for releasing distinctive and unconventional films, and You Can See Everything fits that reputation. The company announced the documentary after keeping the project largely secret. The film's surprise premiere at Telluride added to the sense of mystery surrounding it. Audiences knew very little about the project before the announcement, despite rumors that Nathan Fielder had been interested in Elizabeth Holmes.

The documentary is scheduled to reach theaters in October 2026. Reports following the Telluride announcement have identified October 16, 2026 as the theatrical release date. The film's reported running time is around 174 minutes, making it a substantial documentary rather than a short profile. That length suggests that viewers will get considerably more than the few minutes shown in the initial teaser.

People who already know the Theranos story may wonder whether there is anything new left to say about Elizabeth Holmes. You Can See Everything appears to answer that question by changing the perspective. Instead of concentrating only on the rise and collapse of Theranos, the film focuses on Holmes as a person and on the relationship that develops between her and the filmmakers.

The documentary also raises interesting questions about trust. When a person with a controversial history agrees to let cameras follow her, how much control does she really have over the final story? Can someone who has spent years managing a public image simply become an open documentary subject? And what happens when the filmmaker is Nathan Fielder, whose entire style is built around testing how people behave when situations become strange?

Those questions are likely to make You Can See Everything interesting even for viewers who have never followed the Theranos scandal closely. At its center is a famous entrepreneur, a controversial company and a filmmaker known for pushing documentary conventions. The result could be part business story, part character study and part experiment in observing how a public figure responds when she is placed under an unusually persistent camera.

Elizabeth Holmes has already been portrayed by Amanda Seyfried in the Hulu series The Dropout, while Alex Gibney's documentary The Inventor: Out for Blood in Silicon Valley examined the Theranos scandal. You Can See Everything is different because Holmes herself is participating in the project. That direct access gives Fielder and Oppenheim the opportunity to explore the person behind one of Silicon Valley's most recognizable scandals.

For fans searching for You Can See Everything Nathan Fielder, You Can See Everything movie, You Can See Everything documentary, Nathan Fielder Elizabeth Holmes, or You Can See Everything A24, the main details are now clear. The movie is directed by Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim, centers on Elizabeth Holmes, comes from A24 and is scheduled for a theatrical release in October 2026.

The most interesting part may be what viewers discover after the cameras stay on. Theranos has already been investigated from almost every possible angle, but this documentary approaches Holmes at a very different moment. With Nathan Fielder asking questions and Holmes attempting to explain herself, You Can See Everything could become one of the strangest and most closely watched documentaries of 2026.
