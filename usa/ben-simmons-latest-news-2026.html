Ben Simmons: Latest News, NBA Comeback and 2026 Update

Ben Simmons is once again attracting attention as he prepares for a possible return to the NBA. The former No. 1 overall pick has been away from the NBA since the 2024-25 season and spent the 2025-26 campaign away from the court while dealing with injury issues. Now 30 years old, Simmons has made it clear that he wants another opportunity to play professional basketball.

Simmons was selected first overall by the Philadelphia 76ers in the 2016 NBA Draft after playing one season at Louisiana State University. His NBA debut was delayed because of a foot injury, but when he finally took the court during the 2017-18 season, he immediately showed why Philadelphia had selected him with the top pick.

During his rookie season, Simmons averaged 15.8 points, 8.1 rebounds and 8.2 assists per game. He won the NBA Rookie of the Year award and finished the season with 12 triple-doubles. His combination of size, ball handling, passing ability and defensive versatility made him one of the most unusual young players in the league.

Simmons continued to develop into an All-Star-caliber player. He was selected to three consecutive All-Star teams from 2019 through 2021 and was named to the NBA All-Defensive First Team in 2020 and 2021. He was also selected to the All-NBA Third Team in 2020.

His time in Philadelphia eventually ended after several difficult seasons. Simmons was traded to the Brooklyn Nets in February 2022 in the deal involving James Harden. The move was expected to give Brooklyn another major playmaker, but injuries and a long rehabilitation process prevented him from consistently returning to his previous level.

Back problems became a major part of the story surrounding Simmons. He missed significant periods of basketball and underwent treatment and rehabilitation as he attempted to regain his health. He eventually returned to action for Brooklyn, but his appearances remained limited compared with the expectations created earlier in his career.

In February 2025, Simmons and the Nets reached a contract buyout, allowing him to become a free agent. He subsequently signed with the Los Angeles Clippers. The Clippers announced the signing on February 10, 2025, after Simmons cleared waivers.

When the Clippers signed him, Simmons had appeared in 33 games for Brooklyn during the 2024-25 season. He was averaging 6.2 points, 5.2 rebounds and 6.9 assists in 25 minutes per game. The Clippers viewed his size, passing and ability to defend multiple positions as useful qualities for their roster.

Simmons ultimately appeared in 18 regular-season games for the Clippers during the 2024-25 season. He also saw limited playoff action. After the season, his contract expired and he became an unrestricted free agent.

The biggest development heading into the 2026-27 season is that Simmons wants to make an NBA comeback. He did not play during the 2025-26 NBA season as he concentrated on recovery and rebuilding his body after years of injuries.

In June 2026, Simmons said that he planned to attempt an NBA return. He has been training in Miami and has also spent time working with the Australian national basketball program. His goal is to become physically stronger and show NBA teams that he can contribute again.

Recent reports indicate that Simmons is training without restrictions and is preparing for a return to competitive basketball. He has also expressed an interest in representing Australia at the 2028 Olympic Games in Los Angeles. That would give him another major motivation as he works toward becoming a regular professional player again.

There has been speculation about potential NBA destinations. Simmons has been linked with several teams as a possible low-cost veteran addition, although there has been no confirmed new NBA contract for him at the time of this report. He is currently listed as an unrestricted free agent.

The Miami Heat have received particular attention because Simmons previously identified Miami as a potential destination. However, a preference or reported interest should not be confused with an official agreement. Until a team signs him, his NBA future remains open.

His career numbers explain why teams could still have interest. Simmons has played 383 regular-season games in the NBA and has career averages of 13.1 points, 7.4 rebounds and 7.2 assists per game. Those numbers show the unusual combination of rebounding and playmaking that made him such a valuable prospect early in his career.

At 6 feet 10 inches, Simmons has always had an unusual physical profile for a primary ball handler. He can bring the ball up the floor, defend several positions and create opportunities for teammates. His passing remains one of his most valuable skills, particularly for teams looking for a large playmaker who does not need to dominate possessions through scoring.

His shooting has been one of the most discussed aspects of his career. Simmons has generally been reluctant to take three-point attempts, and opposing defenses have sometimes used that tendency to limit his offensive impact. Any successful comeback will therefore depend not only on his health but also on how coaches use his strengths within modern NBA offenses.

Health is the other major question. Simmons has missed substantial amounts of basketball during the past several seasons, making durability an important factor for any team considering a contract. A healthy training camp and preseason would give interested teams a better opportunity to evaluate what he can provide.

There is also an Australian angle to the comeback story. Simmons has represented Australia internationally and has spoken about his desire to be involved with the Boomers again. Playing for Australia at the 2028 Olympics would be a significant opportunity if he can return to NBA-level form and maintain his health.

For now, the most accurate description of Ben Simmons is a veteran NBA free agent preparing for a comeback. He is not currently under contract with the Clippers, and there is no confirmed new NBA team for him. His next step will depend on his physical condition, workouts and the interest of teams looking for a versatile veteran.

Simmons has already experienced almost every stage of an NBA career, from being the first overall pick and Rookie of the Year to becoming a three-time All-Star, enduring serious injuries and spending an entire season away from the league. His latest training efforts represent another attempt to change the direction of his career.

If Simmons can stay healthy and demonstrate that his athleticism, defense and passing remain at a high level, there could still be a role for him in the NBA. His size and basketball IQ are qualities that cannot easily be taught. The coming months will determine whether those strengths are enough to earn him another NBA contract.
