What Is the Latest Hurricane Forecast?

The hurricane forecast tracks tropical disturbances, tropical depressions, tropical storms and hurricanes that could affect land or marine areas. Forecasters continuously examine satellite imagery, aircraft observations, computer models, ocean temperatures, atmospheric pressure and wind patterns to determine the likely development and movement of tropical systems.

As of August 15, 2026, the National Hurricane Center is providing current tropical weather information for the Atlantic and Pacific basins. Because tropical systems can strengthen or weaken quickly, the forecast should be checked regularly rather than relying on an earlier forecast.

Atlantic Hurricane Outlook

The Atlantic hurricane season is one of the most closely monitored weather periods in the United States, Caribbean and surrounding regions. The official National Hurricane Center Tropical Weather Outlook identifies areas of disturbed weather and estimates their potential for tropical cyclone development.

A disturbance does not necessarily become a tropical storm or hurricane. Weather conditions such as dry air, strong wind shear, cooler water or unfavorable atmospheric circulation can prevent development.

The official outlook is therefore more useful than social-media storm predictions or unofficial computer-model graphics.

Eastern Pacific and Central Pacific Activity

The Eastern Pacific and Central Pacific basins can also experience tropical cyclones during the hurricane season. Systems developing in these regions can affect Mexico, Hawaii and other Pacific locations.

The National Hurricane Center and Central Pacific Hurricane Center monitor these systems and issue official forecasts, watches and warnings when required.

How Hurricane Forecasts Are Made

Modern hurricane forecasting combines multiple sources of information. Satellite observations help meteorologists monitor cloud structure and thunderstorms, while weather models estimate possible future tracks and intensity.

Forecasters also consider ocean temperatures, atmospheric moisture, pressure patterns and upper-level winds. Aircraft reconnaissance can provide valuable observations for certain storms.

  • Satellite imagery: Tracks cloud organization and storm structure.
  • Computer models: Produce possible future tracks and intensity scenarios.
  • Ocean data: Helps determine whether conditions support strengthening.
  • Aircraft observations: Provide detailed information about certain storms.
  • Radar: Helps monitor storms approaching or affecting land.

Hurricane Categories Explained

The Saffir-Simpson Hurricane Wind Scale classifies hurricanes according to their maximum sustained wind speed. Category 1 represents the lowest hurricane category, while Category 5 represents the highest.

Category Maximum Sustained Wind General Risk
Category 1 74–95 mph Dangerous winds
Category 2 96–110 mph Extremely dangerous winds
Category 3 111–129 mph Devastating damage possible
Category 4 130–156 mph Catastrophic damage possible
Category 5 157 mph or higher Catastrophic damage possible

The category describes wind strength only. A lower-category hurricane can still produce dangerous storm surge, flooding, tornadoes and rainfall.

Why the Hurricane Track Can Change

A hurricane forecast track is not a guarantee. Tropical cyclones are influenced by large-scale atmospheric patterns, including high-pressure systems, troughs and steering winds.

Small changes in these patterns can cause a storm to move in a different direction. This is why forecast maps often show a cone representing the probable path of the storm center rather than a single exact line.

Important: The forecast cone does not show the complete area that could experience hurricane conditions. Dangerous wind, rainfall, storm surge and tornadoes can extend far beyond the projected center track.

Hurricane Watches vs Warnings

A hurricane watch means hurricane conditions are possible within the specified area, generally giving residents time to prepare. A hurricane warning means hurricane conditions are expected and people should complete preparations according to local emergency instructions.

Residents should never wait for a warning before beginning preparations if a potentially dangerous storm is approaching.

What to Monitor During a Hurricane

  • Storm track and forecast direction.

  • Storm surge forecasts.
  • Flooding and rainfall warnings.
  • Tornado threats associated with landfalling systems.
  • Local evacuation orders.
  • Emergency alerts from local authorities.

Hurricane Preparation Checklist

  • Keep several days of drinking water available.
  • Prepare non-perishable food.
  • Charge phones and backup batteries.
  • Keep important documents protected.
  • Secure loose outdoor objects.
  • Monitor official weather alerts.
  • Know the local evacuation route.
  • Follow instructions from emergency authorities.

Hurricane Forecast and Climate

Hurricane seasons can vary significantly from year to year. Seasonal forecasts provide an estimate of overall activity, but they cannot determine exactly where an individual hurricane will make landfall months in advance.

For people living in hurricane-prone regions, preparation is therefore more important than attempting to predict one particular storm far ahead of time.
