Winter 2026-2027 snowfall predictions as exceptional El Niño strengthens

Winter 2026-2027 is attracting unusual attention from weather watchers because a strengthening El Niño is expected to influence weather patterns across the Northern Hemisphere. The latest information from the World Meteorological Organization says El Niño is firmly established and is expected to strengthen further, with nearly a 100 percent likelihood that it will persist through the December 2026 to February 2027 period.

The developing event is being described by international forecasters as an exceptionally strong El Niño. That does not mean every location will experience extreme winter weather, and it does not allow forecasters to predict the exact amount of snow that will fall in a particular city months in advance. Instead, El Niño changes the large-scale atmospheric pattern, increasing the odds of certain types of temperature and precipitation conditions in different regions.

NOAA's Climate Prediction Center reported in August that El Niño was strengthening and gave a greater than 90 percent chance of a very strong event during the Northern Hemisphere fall and winter of 2026-2027. NOAA also said there was a 69 percent chance during October through December of an event reaching a historically very strong threshold. These probabilities make the upcoming winter especially important for seasonal weather forecasting.

El Niño is a recurring climate pattern involving unusually warm ocean surface temperatures in the central and eastern tropical Pacific Ocean. The warmer water affects the atmosphere above it, changing atmospheric circulation, rainfall and the position of the jet streams. Those changes can then influence weather thousands of miles away from the Pacific.

One important point is that El Niño does not simply mean a warmer winter everywhere. Its effects are regional. Some areas can experience warmer conditions while other regions become cooler or wetter. Some places may see more snow because of increased precipitation, while other areas can experience less snow if temperatures are too warm for precipitation to remain frozen.

For the United States, NOAA's seasonal outlook provides some useful clues. The agency expects El Niño's influence to become increasingly important as winter approaches. During the core winter period, above-normal temperatures are favored across much of the northern tier of the country, with probabilities above 50 percent extending from the Pacific Northwest toward the Northeast.

The southern part of the country has a different signal. NOAA expects El Niño to increase the chances of above-normal precipitation across parts of the southern United States as winter progresses. The precipitation signal becomes particularly notable across California and the Southeast, where the probability of wetter-than-normal conditions increases during the winter and into early spring.

This distinction is important when discussing snowfall predictions. More precipitation does not automatically mean more snow. Snow requires sufficiently cold air as well as moisture. A location that receives heavy precipitation while temperatures remain above freezing could see rain instead of snow.

California is one area receiving particular attention. NOAA's winter outlook says above-normal precipitation probabilities increase significantly across much of coastal California and adjacent parts of southern Arizona during December through February and into February through April. The strongest signal is expected during January through March.

Historical El Niño events provide some context, although they cannot be used as a precise forecast for individual cities. NOAA noted that recent strong eastern-Pacific El Niño events have produced substantial precipitation along the West Coast, particularly during late winter. Parts of California experienced exceptionally wet February conditions during previous major events.

The Southeast is another region where the precipitation signal is important. NOAA's outlook indicates that probabilities of above-normal precipitation can exceed 60 percent across parts of the Southeast during November through January and remain elevated into early spring. That could mean a wetter winter, but whether individual storms produce snow, ice or rain will depend on local temperatures and short-term weather patterns.

The Northeast presents a more complicated picture. El Niño can influence the jet stream and storm track, but the resulting winter pattern is not identical every year. NOAA's seasonal temperature outlook favors above-normal temperatures across much of the northern tier, which could limit snow opportunities in some areas even when individual winter storms are capable of producing significant snowfall.

Snowfall predictions therefore need to be treated differently from seasonal temperature predictions. A seasonal outlook can identify broad tendencies, but it cannot accurately predict whether a particular town will receive a snowstorm on a specific date several months ahead. Those details become much more reliable when shorter-range forecasts are available.

The strength of the 2026 El Niño is nevertheless significant. WMO's September 3 update says exceptionally warm tropical Pacific Ocean conditions are supporting further intensification. It also says a very strong El Niño can significantly shift rainfall and temperature patterns worldwide and increase the potential for extreme weather.

The effects will not be limited to North America. El Niño is one of the major natural drivers of global climate variability. Changes in Pacific Ocean temperatures can influence rainfall patterns in South America, parts of Asia, Australia and Africa, as well as temperature patterns across many regions.

Some regions may face increased rainfall and flooding risks, while others can experience drought conditions. El Niño can also influence tropical cyclone activity. NOAA has previously explained that El Niño tends to increase vertical wind shear over the Atlantic, which can suppress the development and organisation of Atlantic hurricanes.

The 2026-2027 winter is also being watched because the developing El Niño is occurring alongside exceptionally warm global ocean conditions. WMO has warned that the combination can contribute to significant shifts in weather patterns and increase the likelihood of weather extremes.

For people interested specifically in winter snowfall, the safest conclusion at this stage is that the coming season could produce very different outcomes depending on location. Parts of the southern United States and West Coast have an increased likelihood of wetter conditions, while much of the northern tier has a warmer-than-normal seasonal signal. That combination can create opportunities for major snow events in some areas without guaranteeing a snowy winter overall.

Another important factor is timing. El Niño typically reaches its greatest influence during the Northern Hemisphere winter. WMO says the phenomenon normally peaks between November and February. That means forecasts issued closer to December, January and February should provide a clearer picture of individual regional winter conditions than forecasts made many months in advance.

The current forecast should therefore not be interpreted as a prediction that every location will have a record-breaking winter. Instead, it points toward an unusual large-scale climate setup that could produce stronger-than-normal weather contrasts between regions.

For anyone planning travel, outdoor activities, agriculture or winter preparation, local weather forecasts will remain essential. Seasonal El Niño forecasts provide background information, while medium-range and short-range forecasts are needed to determine actual snowstorms, freezing temperatures, heavy rain and other specific events.

The key fact for winter 2026-2027 is that El Niño is no longer merely a possibility. It is established and strengthening. WMO expects it to persist through the core winter period, while NOAA has assigned a very high probability to a very strong event. The resulting weather pattern could make the winter season notably different across various parts of the world.

Snow lovers will have to wait for shorter-range forecasts before knowing where the biggest storms will develop. However, the broad seasonal signals already suggest that the 2026-2027 winter deserves close attention, particularly across the United States, where El Niño can strongly influence the position of the jet stream, storm tracks, precipitation and temperature.

As new seasonal outlooks are released, the forecast picture should become clearer. NOAA's next scheduled set of long-lead outlooks is due in September, providing another opportunity to assess how the exceptional El Niño pattern is developing and what it could mean for the remainder of winter.
