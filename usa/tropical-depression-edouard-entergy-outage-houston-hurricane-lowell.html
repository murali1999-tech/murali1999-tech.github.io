Tropical Depression Edouard Brings Flooding and Power Outages to Texas as Hurricane Lowell Strengthens

Tropical Depression Edouard is continuing to affect eastern and southeastern Texas after moving inland from the Gulf Coast. The system previously strengthened into Tropical Storm Edouard before making landfall near Johnson Bayou, Louisiana, close to the Texas border, on Tuesday, September 1. It made landfall with maximum sustained winds of about 60 mph before weakening after moving farther inland.

As of Wednesday, September 2, Edouard had weakened to a tropical depression. The National Hurricane Center reported the center of the system near 30.5 degrees north and 94.6 degrees west, about 40 miles northwest of Beaumont and roughly 50 miles southeast of Lufkin. Maximum sustained winds were around 35 mph, with stronger gusts possible.

The biggest concern is no longer hurricane-force wind. Heavy rainfall and flash flooding have become the main threats. Some parts of southeast Texas have received extremely high rainfall totals, with reports of more than 20 inches in localized areas. The combination of already saturated ground, additional rain and rising water levels has created dangerous conditions in several communities.

A flash flood emergency was issued for parts of Hardin and Tyler counties as intense rainfall affected the region. Roads have flooded, trees have fallen and some traffic signals have stopped working. Residents in areas affected by flooding have been warned to avoid unnecessary travel and never drive through water covering a roadway.

The Houston area is also watching Edouard closely. Houston has not received the same level of rainfall as some communities closer to Beaumont and the Piney Woods, but additional rain can still create localized flooding. Areas north and east of Houston have faced a higher flood risk, and residents should continue monitoring local weather alerts.

Power outages are another major concern. Thousands of customers across southeast Texas have lost electricity as strong winds, heavy rain, flooding and falling trees affected the electrical system. Reports Wednesday morning indicated that tens of thousands of customers remained without power across several Texas counties.

For people searching for the Entergy outage map, the outage tracker can help customers check whether their neighborhood has an active outage and whether a restoration estimate has been provided. Entergy Texas has crews working to restore service in communities affected by Edouard.

CenterPoint Energy customers in the greater Houston region can also check the company's outage information and report an outage. CenterPoint has crews positioned to respond to weather-related damage, while residents should stay away from downed electrical lines and damaged electrical equipment.

If you are searching for a power outage near me, the most reliable information will normally come from the electricity provider serving your address. Outage maps can change quickly during severe weather because new outages may be reported while crews are restoring earlier ones.

Residents should also check school closings before leaving home. Several school districts in areas affected by flooding and power problems have announced cancellations or schedule changes. School closures can vary by district, so parents and students should check the official website or social media account of their individual school district for the latest information.

Houston-area residents should also be prepared for changing road conditions. Even if rain appears lighter in the city, heavy rainfall farther north or east can contribute to flooding downstream. Low-lying roads, underpasses, drainage channels and areas near creeks can become dangerous quickly.

The National Hurricane Center has issued its final advisory on Edouard as a tropical cyclone because the system is weakening inland. Future information concerning the remaining flood threat is being handled through the Weather Prediction Center and local National Weather Service offices.

Edouard is expected to continue weakening as it moves across eastern Texas. The system is forecast to become a remnant low and then dissipate. Even after the tropical cyclone itself disappears, flooding can continue because water takes time to drain from saturated ground and swollen waterways.

Meanwhile, another tropical system is attracting attention in the Pacific. Hurricane Lowell has rapidly strengthened and has become a major hurricane. The storm is located well away from the mainland United States, but its large swells may affect parts of Hawaii.

Reports from the Central Pacific indicate that Lowell has strengthened significantly, with maximum sustained winds reported around 145 mph in the latest forecasts. The storm is expected to remain offshore, but dangerous surf and rip currents can still become a problem even when the center of a hurricane does not make landfall.

People in Hawaii should therefore pay attention to local beach and ocean safety warnings. Large swells can create dangerous conditions for swimmers, surfers and people near rocky coastlines. Conditions at beaches can become hazardous before a storm is anywhere close to the islands.

The term hurricane center is commonly used when people are looking for the latest official storm information. The National Hurricane Center provides official forecasts and advisories for tropical systems in the Atlantic and eastern Pacific, while the Central Pacific Hurricane Center provides information for systems affecting the central Pacific and Hawaii.

For residents in Texas, the immediate issue remains flooding and power restoration rather than hurricane-force winds. Anyone affected by Edouard should keep phones charged, have flashlights available and avoid flooded roads. If electricity is out, refrigerators and freezers should remain closed as much as possible to preserve food for longer.

People should also be careful around damaged trees and electrical equipment. Downed power lines can remain dangerous even when they appear inactive. Residents should report dangerous electrical conditions to their utility provider or emergency authorities rather than approaching the equipment themselves.

The situation can change throughout the day as Edouard's rainfall moves farther inland. Houston, Beaumont, Port Arthur, Lufkin and surrounding communities should continue checking official weather warnings, local emergency announcements, school district updates and utility outage maps.

For the latest storm information, residents should rely on the National Hurricane Center, National Weather Service, local emergency management agencies and their electricity provider. Social media reports can provide useful local observations, but official warnings should take priority when decisions about travel, flooding or personal safety are involved.
