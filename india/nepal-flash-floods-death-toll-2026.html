Nepal Flash Floods Death Toll: Latest Facts and Figures

As of August 30, 2026, the combined death toll from the catastrophic flash floods and landslides in Nepal and China's Tibet region has reached 750, according to figures reported by authorities. Nepal has reported 734 deaths, while Chinese authorities have reported 16 deaths in Tibet. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

Nepal's disaster authority reported 2,498 people missing in the country. In Tibet, Chinese authorities reported another 546 people missing. The combined number of missing people is 3,044. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

The disaster began on August 26 after a massive flow of water, ice, rocks and debris swept through communities near the Nepal-China border. The flooding caused severe destruction along river valleys and affected hydropower facilities and other infrastructure. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

More than 8,000 people had been rescued or recovered in Nepal according to figures reported by Nepal's disaster authorities. Rescue operations have been complicated by heavy mud, damaged roads, difficult mountain terrain and continuing bad weather. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

Nepal has reported 933 hydropower workers among those missing. More than 100 workers are believed to be trapped inside tunnels at several hydropower projects, including facilities along the Trishuli River. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

The missing-person figures also include foreign nationals. Nepal's disaster authority reported 589 foreign nationals among those unaccounted for. Chinese authorities separately reported 261 foreign nationals missing in Tibet. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

At least 49 Indian citizens were reported among the 261 foreign nationals missing in the Tibet area, according to officials cited by the Times of India. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}

The flooding has affected more than 90,000 people in Nepal, according to estimates cited by the International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies. :contentReference[oaicite:7]{index=7}

Rescue teams from Nepal, India and China have been involved in efforts to locate missing people and reach workers trapped in tunnels. India has deployed a specialised tunnel-rescue team, while China has also sent specialists. :contentReference[oaicite:8]{index=8}

The disaster has also created concerns about additional flooding. Nepal issued warnings on August 30 after water levels in the Bhotekoshi River increased. Authorities urged people in affected river areas and rescue personnel to remain cautious. :contentReference[oaicite:9]{index=9}

Officials and scientists have linked the disaster to a major glacier and rock collapse in the Himalayan region, which sent a destructive torrent downstream. Search and rescue operations remain underway, so casualty and missing-person figures may change as authorities identify victims and locate survivors. :contentReference[oaicite:10]{index=10}
