Diwali, also known as Deepavali, is one of the most celebrated Hindu festivals in India. The festival represents the victory of light over darkness and good over evil. People celebrate this special occasion by decorating their homes, lighting diyas, worshipping Goddess Lakshmi and Lord Ganesha, exchanging sweets and spending time with family members.

Diwali 2026 will be celebrated on Sunday, November 8, according to the commonly followed calendar. Lakshmi Puja is an important part of the celebration. Devotees worship Goddess Lakshmi for happiness, prosperity, wealth and peace in their homes. The evening is considered especially important for Lakshmi Pujan.

For Hyderabad, the Lakshmi Puja muhurat is approximately 6:07 PM to 8:08 PM on November 8, 2026. The timing is based on local Panchang calculations and should be checked against a reliable local calendar before performing the puja. Timings can differ between cities because of geographical location and sunrise and sunset calculations.

Diwali Lakshmi Puja is traditionally performed during Pradosh Kaal. Devotees clean their homes, decorate the entrance with rangoli, light oil lamps and prepare flowers, fruits, sweets and other offerings. A clean and peaceful environment is considered important for worship.

Families generally begin the puja by remembering Lord Ganesha. Lord Ganesha is worshipped before other deities as the remover of obstacles. Goddess Lakshmi is then worshipped with flowers, kumkum, turmeric, incense, lamps and devotional prayers. Some families also worship Goddess Saraswati and Lord Kubera according to their traditions.

For Lakshmi Puja, devotees can arrange an image or idol of Goddess Lakshmi and Lord Ganesha on a clean platform. Light a diya and incense sticks. Offer flowers, fruits, sweets and other traditional naivedyam. Apply kumkum and turmeric and offer prayers with devotion. Recite Lakshmi mantras or Lakshmi Ashtakam if familiar with them. Finally, perform Lakshmi Aarti and seek blessings for the well-being of the family.

One commonly recited Lakshmi mantra is Om Shreem Mahalakshmyai Namah. Devotees chant this mantra with faith and concentration. Many families also recite Lakshmi Chalisa, Lakshmi Ashtakam and other traditional prayers during Diwali.

Goddess Lakshmi is traditionally associated with wealth, prosperity, good fortune and abundance. However, Diwali is not only about financial prosperity. It is also a reminder to welcome positive thoughts, kindness, generosity and harmony into everyday life.

After the puja, families perform aarti together. Lakshmi Mata Aarti is one of the most popular devotional songs sung during Diwali. Devotees sing the aarti while moving a lit lamp in front of the deity. Ganesh Ji Ki Aarti is also performed to seek the blessings of Lord Ganesha.

Diwali celebrations also include lighting diyas in balconies, windows, entrances and courtyards. Children enjoy sweets and festive decorations. Many families visit relatives, exchange gifts and share traditional food. People also decorate their homes with colourful rangoli designs and flowers.

The five main days associated with Diwali celebrations include Dhanteras, Naraka Chaturdashi, Diwali, Govardhan Puja and Bhai Dooj. In 2026, Dhanteras is observed on November 6. The main Diwali celebrations take place on November 8 according to the calendar followed here. Govardhan Puja and Bhai Dooj follow on subsequent days, depending on regional Panchang observances.

Dhanteras is traditionally associated with wealth, health and prosperity. People purchase household items, utensils and precious metals according to their customs. Naraka Chaturdashi commemorates the victory of Lord Krishna over Narakasura. Diwali is celebrated with Lakshmi Puja and the lighting of lamps. Govardhan Puja remembers Lord Krishna lifting Govardhan Hill, and Bhai Dooj celebrates the loving relationship between brothers and sisters.

A Panchang calculated for one city should not automatically be used for another location.

Diwali is a festival that brings families and communities together. The lighting of a small diya carries a beautiful message: even a little light can remove darkness. May this Deepavali bring happiness, peace, good health and prosperity to every home. Happy Diwali 2026!
