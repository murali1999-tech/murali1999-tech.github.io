Raksha Bandhan 2026: Rakhi Muhurat, 28 August Chandra Grahan Time in India

Raksha Bandhan 2026 will be celebrated on Friday, August 28, across India. The festival falls on the Shravana Purnima and is traditionally observed by sisters tying a rakhi around their brothers' wrists and praying for their happiness, health and long life. This year, many people are searching for the correct rakhi muhurat because a lunar eclipse also occurs on August 28.

According to Panchang calculations, the Purnima Tithi begins on August 27 and continues until approximately 9:48 AM on August 28. The commonly published Raksha Bandhan thread ceremony window for New Delhi is 5:57 AM to 9:48 AM on August 28. The other major event on the same date is the August 28, 2026 lunar eclipse, known in Hindi as Chandra Grahan. Astronomically, it is a deep partial lunar eclipse. There is an important point for people in India: although the eclipse occurs during Indian daytime hours, the Moon will not be above the horizon in India during the visible eclipse phases. Therefore, the August 28 lunar eclipse is not visible from India. For families following traditional customs, the basic Raksha Bandhan ceremony usually involves preparing a puja thali with the rakhi, roli or kumkum, rice or akshat, flowers, sweets and a diya. The brother is traditionally seated before the sister performs the customary tilak and ties the rakhi.

The meaning of Raksha Bandhan goes beyond the thread itself. The rakhi represents affection, protection and the continuing bond between siblings. Sisters traditionally pray for their brothers' well-being, while brothers promise to support and protect their sisters. Modern families often celebrate the same relationship even when siblings live far apart.

The 2026 festival is especially interesting because of the coincidence with the lunar eclipse. The key distinction is between when the eclipse occurs and whether it is visible from a particular location.

Scientific eclipse timings describe the movement of the Moon through Earth's shadow. Religious observances involving Sutak and rituals are based on traditional customs and may differ between communities. Therefore, astronomical timing and religious muhurat should not be treated as exactly the same thing.
