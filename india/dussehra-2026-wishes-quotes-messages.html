Dussehra, also known as Vijayadashami, is one of the most celebrated Hindu festivals in India. It represents the victory of good over evil and reminds people that truth, courage and righteousness can overcome even the greatest challenges. Families, friends and communities celebrate the occasion with prayers, traditional rituals, cultural programmes and heartfelt greetings.

Dussehra 2026 falls on Tuesday, October 20, according to commonly followed Hindu festival calendars. The festival is celebrated on the tenth day of the bright half of the Hindu lunar month of Ashwin. In many places, people worship Goddess Durga, remember Lord Rama's victory over Ravana, and seek blessings for happiness, health and prosperity.

Puja timings can vary depending on the city, local sunrise and the regional calendar. People planning Dussehra puja should check a reliable local panchang for the Vijay Muhurat and other auspicious timings applicable to their location.

For children, Dussehra is a wonderful opportunity to learn about the stories of Lord Rama and Goddess Durga. Families may attend local celebrations, enjoy cultural performances and explain the importance of courage, respect and doing what is right.

Traditional Dussehra celebrations differ across India. In some places, large effigies of Ravana are burned to symbolise the defeat of evil. In other regions, the festival concludes Navaratri celebrations and honours Goddess Durga's victory over Mahishasura. In Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, families may observe Vijayadashami with prayers, temple visits and other regional customs.
