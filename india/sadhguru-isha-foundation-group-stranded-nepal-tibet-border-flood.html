Sadhguru Isha Foundation Group Missing Near Nepal-Tibet Border After Devastating Flash Flood

A group of pilgrims associated with the Isha Foundation, founded by spiritual leader Jaggi Vasudev, popularly known as Sadhguru, remains unaccounted for after a devastating flash flood struck the Gyirong area of Tibet near the Nepal-China border. The incident has triggered an urgent search operation and left families waiting for information about their relatives.

The group was travelling as part of a Kailash Mansarovar pilgrimage. According to reports, the pilgrims were returning after completing the pilgrimage and were at the Gyirong immigration centre while completing formalities to return towards Nepal and Kathmandu when the flash flood struck on August 26, 2026.

Current reports put the number of missing Isha Foundation pilgrims at 77. Earlier reports had referred to the group as around 80 people because the travelling party also included volunteers and a doctor who had gone to assist the pilgrims. The group included people from 19 nationalities, according to the Isha Foundation coordinator quoted in news reports.

The location of the incident is important because Gyirong lies in Tibet close to the Nepal border and is an important crossing point for people travelling through the region. The sudden flood caused extensive destruction, damaged infrastructure and severely disrupted communications and access routes.

According to reports, the group lost contact after the disaster. As of August 29, there had been no confirmed information about the whereabouts of the 77 pilgrims for two days. Rescue teams were working in the affected area, but the destruction and difficult terrain were making the search extremely challenging.

Sadhguru has expressed deep concern about the group. Reports say he wanted to travel towards Gyirong to assist with relief and rescue efforts, but he was unable to obtain the necessary permission to enter the severely affected area. Roads leading towards the location were also damaged or cut off, making access difficult.

Indian, Nepalese and Chinese authorities, along with diplomatic missions and embassies, have been contacted as efforts continue to locate the missing pilgrims. Families are also waiting for official information about their relatives. The lack of communication from the disaster-hit region has increased anxiety among those waiting for news.

The flash flood itself caused widespread destruction on both sides of the Nepal-Tibet border. The disaster affected roads, bridges, buildings and other infrastructure. Rescue teams have faced difficult weather and terrain while trying to reach areas that were heavily damaged by water, mud and debris.

On August 29, reports said Chinese rescuers had reached the Gyirong immigration centre after several days of difficult access. The area was described as severely devastated, with the border complex largely destroyed. This development is significant because the immigration centre was the location where several pilgrims were believed to have been present when the flood struck.

It is important to distinguish between confirmed information and speculation. The 77 Isha Foundation pilgrims are currently reported as missing or unaccounted for. Their deaths have not been officially confirmed in the reports cited here. Rescue and search operations are continuing, and authorities are still working to determine the fate and location of the group.

The wider disaster has affected thousands of people in Nepal and Tibet. Many other foreign tourists and pilgrims were also caught in the floods while travelling in the region. The Kailash Mansarovar pilgrimage route has therefore become one of the areas receiving significant attention from families, diplomatic missions and rescue authorities.

The incident has also highlighted the risks of travelling through remote Himalayan areas during extreme weather. Mountain regions can experience sudden floods, landslides and other hazards, and once roads and communication networks are destroyed, rescue teams can face major difficulties reaching stranded people.

For the families of the missing Isha Foundation pilgrims, the priority remains receiving verified information. Officials and rescue teams are continuing efforts to locate people affected by the disaster. Until authorities provide confirmation, reports about the fate of individual pilgrims should be treated carefully.

As of August 29, 2026, the key confirmed information is that 77 Isha Foundation pilgrims remain unaccounted for following the August 26 flash flood at Gyirong near the Nepal-Tibet border. The group was returning from the Kailash Mansarovar pilgrimage, and rescue operations and efforts to establish contact are continuing.
