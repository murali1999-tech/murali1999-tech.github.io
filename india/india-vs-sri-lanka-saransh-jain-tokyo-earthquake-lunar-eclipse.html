Tokyo Earthquake Today

A magnitude 5.9 earthquake struck eastern Japan early on Sunday, August 23, 2026. The earthquake occurred in the southern part of Ibaraki Prefecture at a depth of approximately 70 kilometres and was felt across the greater Tokyo region.

Reports indicated shaking in Tokyo and surrounding parts of the Kanto region. Transport services were affected, and emergency authorities monitored the situation for injuries and damage.

Importantly, reports stated that there was no tsunami threat associated with the earthquake. Authorities nevertheless advised people to remain alert because aftershocks can occur following a significant earthquake.

Earthquake safety: During strong shaking, move away from windows and objects that can fall. If indoors, take cover under sturdy furniture or protect your head and neck. Follow instructions from Japanese authorities and local emergency services.

Earthquake information can change rapidly as authorities update magnitude estimates, injury numbers and damage assessments. Readers should therefore rely on the Japan Meteorological Agency and other established news organisations for the latest information.

Lunar Eclipses in 2026

Lunar eclipses happen when the Earth moves between the Sun and the Moon and the Moon passes through Earth's shadow. Unlike a solar eclipse, a lunar eclipse can be observed safely with the naked eye from locations where the eclipse is visible.

The year 2026 has two lunar eclipses. The first was a total lunar eclipse in March. The second will be a deep partial lunar eclipse occurring on August 27–28.

The August 2026 event is particularly interesting because approximately 96 percent of the Moon's disc will pass through Earth's darker umbral shadow. This makes it a very deep partial lunar eclipse, with only a small portion of the Moon remaining outside the umbra at maximum eclipse.

During a lunar eclipse, the eclipsed portion of the Moon can take on a reddish, copper or orange appearance. This happens because sunlight passing through Earth's atmosphere is filtered and scattered before reaching the Moon.

Visibility depends on location and the Moon's position above the horizon. The August 27–28 eclipse will be visible from parts of the Americas, Europe, Africa and other regions, while it will not be directly visible from India according to published visibility maps.
