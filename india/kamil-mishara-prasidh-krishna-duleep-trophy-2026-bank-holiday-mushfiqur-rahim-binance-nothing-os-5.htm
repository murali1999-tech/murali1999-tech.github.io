Ekadashi Vrat Katha

Sawan Putrada Ekadashi is being observed on August 23, 2026, although some regional calendars may place observance on August 24 because of differences in tithi calculations and local traditions.

Ekadashi is traditionally dedicated to Lord Vishnu. Devotees observe fasting, prayer, Vishnu nama japa and the reading or listening of the Ekadashi Vrat Katha.

Putrada Ekadashi Vrat Katha

According to the traditional story associated with Putrada Ekadashi, King Mahijit was a righteous ruler who was unable to have children. His subjects sought guidance from sages to discover the reason for his difficulty.

Sage Lomesh explained that the king had committed an act in a previous birth that had resulted in the condition he was experiencing. The sage advised the king and his subjects to observe Putrada Ekadashi with devotion and listen to its sacred story.

The king and queen followed the prescribed observances and prayed to Lord Vishnu. In the traditional account, their devotion was rewarded with the birth of a son.

Traditional significance: Putrada Ekadashi is associated with prayers for family welfare, children, prosperity and spiritual purification. The story is a traditional religious narrative rather than a scientifically established claim.

Devotees who observe the vrat generally follow their family or regional calendar for fasting rules, paran time and dietary restrictions.

Lunar Eclipses 2026

One of the most interesting astronomical events of August 2026 will be the deep partial lunar eclipse occurring on August 27-28. NASA describes it as a deep partial lunar eclipse as the Moon passes through Earth's penumbra and umbra.

The eclipse begins with the penumbral phase before the Moon enters Earth's darker umbral shadow. At maximum eclipse, a large portion of the Moon will be inside the umbra, producing a noticeable change in the appearance of the lunar surface.

For a location in the Americas, the published local schedule shows the penumbral eclipse beginning at 9:23 p.m., the partial phase beginning at 10:33 p.m., maximum eclipse at 12:12 a.m., the partial phase ending at 1:51 a.m. and the penumbral phase ending at 3:01 a.m.

Exact local visibility and timings depend on location. The event should therefore be checked using an astronomy source that provides location-specific calculations.

August 27-28, 2026: NASA classifies the event as a deep partial lunar eclipse.
