Volcanoes are another subject that regularly attracts worldwide attention. An eruption can produce lava, ash, volcanic gases and powerful explosions, depending on the type of volcano and the nature of the activity. Even when lava does not threaten nearby settlements, ash can affect air quality, roads, crops and aircraft operations.

Scientists monitor volcanoes using instruments that measure earthquakes, ground deformation, gas emissions and temperature changes. These observations can help authorities identify changes in volcanic activity, although predicting the exact timing and size of an eruption remains difficult. People living near an active volcano should follow evacuation instructions and official hazard alerts rather than relying on unverified videos or rumours.

Not every plume of smoke or cloud above a mountain indicates a major eruption. Some volcanic activity involves steam and gas emissions, while other events may include explosive ash releases or lava flows. Readers seeking current information can consult the United States Geological Survey Volcano Hazards Program and the relevant national geological authority for the volcano concerned.

Cold sores are a common health concern and usually appear as small, painful blisters around the lips or mouth. They are generally caused by the herpes simplex virus. Some people notice tingling, itching or a burning sensation before a blister develops. The blisters may later break, form a crust and gradually heal.

Cold sores are contagious from the first warning signs until they have completely healed. To reduce the chance of spreading the infection, avoid kissing while a sore is present, do not share lip balm and wash your hands before and after applying treatment. Touching the sore unnecessarily can also spread the virus to other parts of the body.

A pharmacist may recommend an antiviral cream or a product to ease discomfort. Antiviral treatment tends to be most useful when started at the earliest tingling stage. Cold sores commonly clear up within around ten days, although recovery varies from person to person. A healthcare professional should be consulted if a sore is unusually large or painful, has not begun healing after ten days, or if the person has a weakened immune system. Advice is available from the NHS cold sores guide.
