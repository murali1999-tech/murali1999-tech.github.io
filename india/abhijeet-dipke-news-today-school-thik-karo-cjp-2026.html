Abhijeet Dipke News Today: Latest Updates on CJP Founder and School Thik Karo Campaign

Updated: August 18, 2026
Abhijeet Dipke latest news: Abhijeet Dipke, founder of the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP), is back in the news today over his campaign on government schools and rural education. His recent activities have focused on school infrastructure, transportation for students in remote areas and accountability in the public education system.

Abhijeet Dipke has emerged as one of the prominent young voices in India's current education and youth-related political discussions. The CJP founder has recently shifted attention toward government schools through his "School Thik Karo" campaign, which began in his native village in Maharashtra's Hingoli district.

The campaign has generated attention because Dipke is seeking to highlight problems that students and teachers face in government schools. Recent reports say the campaign has moved beyond school buildings and facilities to a wider issue: whether children in rural and remote areas have reliable access to education.

Abhijeet Dipke News Today

One of the main Abhijeet Dipke news developments today concerns his call for ministers, MLAs and MPs to send their children to government schools for a day. Dipke has argued that policymakers should experience the difficulties faced by children in rural areas, including long journeys to school and poor transportation facilities.

During a protest in Hingoli, Dipke highlighted the difficulties faced by students who live in remote locations. According to reports, some children may have to walk several kilometres to attend school, with the problem becoming more serious during the monsoon season when roads can become muddy or waterlogged.

Dipke has questioned why governments can arrange large-scale transportation for political events while students in remote areas continue to face difficulties reaching their schools.

Today's key update: Dipke's "School Thik Karo" campaign is now focusing on rural school access and transportation, rather than only infrastructure problems inside school buildings.

What Is the School Thik Karo Campaign?

The "School Thik Karo" campaign was launched by Abhijeet Dipke on August 15, 2026, from his native village in Hingoli, Maharashtra. The initiative is intended to draw attention to the condition of government schools and encourage greater public participation in identifying and resolving problems.

During an initial inspection of a government school, Dipke reportedly pointed to several basic infrastructure problems. These included damaged windows, inadequate seating, problems with toilets and a lack of basic water facilities.

The campaign has subsequently received attention after repair work began at the school. Reports also said that the school received its first-ever computer system following the campaign, adding a technology-related dimension to the effort.

Government School Gets Computer After Dipke Campaign

A significant development associated with Dipke's campaign is the installation of a computer at a government school in his native village. The development was presented as an example of how public attention and community involvement can lead to improvements in government institutions.

The focus on computers is particularly important because access to digital resources is increasingly connected with education. Students in rural schools can face disadvantages when they do not have access to computers, internet connectivity, laboratories or other educational technology.

Dipke's campaign therefore combines concerns about basic infrastructure with broader questions about educational opportunity. A school needs functioning classrooms, toilets, drinking water and seating, but students also increasingly require access to technology and modern learning resources.

Why Abhijeet Dipke Is Trending

His recent shift toward government-school conditions has created another major discussion. Instead of limiting the campaign to political speeches, Dipke has been visiting schools, documenting problems and demanding action from local authorities.

Abhijeet Dipke and Rural Education

Rural education has become a major theme in Dipke's latest campaign. Government schools in rural areas can face challenges that are different from those experienced by schools in large cities. Transportation, teacher availability, infrastructure, digital access and basic amenities can all affect attendance and learning.

Students living far from schools may have to travel long distances. During heavy rains, the journey can become even more difficult. Dipke has used these issues to argue that policymakers should personally understand the conditions experienced by students.

His call for political leaders to send their children to government schools is designed to create a debate about confidence in the public education system. The argument is that if decision-makers have greater personal involvement with government schools, pressure to improve those institutions could increase.

Expansion of School Thik Karo

Dipke has indicated that the campaign could expand beyond his native village. The broader objective is to encourage communities to examine government schools and identify problems that require attention.

A social-audit approach could involve parents, teachers, students and local communities. Such participation can help identify problems that may not always be visible through official reports.

The campaign is expected to focus on multiple talukas in Maharashtra, with a larger national ambition. Whether the initiative develops into a nationwide movement will depend on participation from local communities and the response of government authorities.

Who Is Abhijeet Dipke?

Abhijeet Dipke is a political communications strategist and the founder of the Cockroach Janta Party. He became widely known in 2026 through a youth-led protest movement that focused on examination issues, unemployment and concerns about India's education system.

Dipke's recent School Thik Karo campaign represents a shift toward local and grassroots issues. Instead of concentrating only on national-level political demands, he is now drawing attention to problems inside individual government schools and rural communities.
