Abhinandan Varthaman Joins FLY91 as Commercial Pilot After IAF Retirement

Group Captain Abhinandan Varthaman, one of India's best-known military pilots, has started a new chapter in aviation after retiring from the Indian Air Force. The Vir Chakra awardee has joined Goa-based regional airline FLY91 as a commercial pilot, according to reports published on September 3, 2026.

The move marks a major change in the career of Abhinandan Varthaman. After more than two decades in military aviation, he has moved from flying fighter aircraft for the IAF to civilian aviation. Reports say he retired from the Indian Air Force on March 31, 2026, after around 22 years of service, and joined FLY91 in August.

Abhinandan became internationally known after the India-Pakistan aerial engagement of February 2019. He was flying a MiG-21 Bison during the confrontation that followed India's Balakot air operation. Indian authorities said he shot down a Pakistani F-16 before his own aircraft was hit and he ejected over territory controlled by Pakistan.

After ejecting, Abhinandan was captured by Pakistani forces. He was held for three days before being returned to India through the Wagah-Attari border crossing on March 1, 2019. His calm conduct after returning to India made him a widely recognized figure across the country.

For his actions during the 2019 aerial engagement, Abhinandan was awarded the Vir Chakra, India's third-highest wartime gallantry award. He was later promoted to Group Captain, adding another milestone to a distinguished Air Force career.

The decision to move into commercial aviation is not unusual for former military pilots. Fighter pilots leave the armed forces with extensive experience in aircraft operations, navigation, aviation safety, emergency procedures and disciplined decision-making. Many former military aviators eventually move into civilian airline careers after completing the required regulatory and airline training.

In Abhinandan's case, the transition takes him to FLY91, a relatively young Indian regional airline. FLY91 is based in Goa and operates regional services using ATR 72-600 aircraft. The airline began commercial operations in March 2024 and has expanded its network to connect smaller cities with larger aviation hubs.

Reports say Abhinandan joined the airline in August, although FLY91 has not publicly disclosed detailed information about his employment or specific flying responsibilities. The company has cited employee privacy when asked about personal details.

This means that while Abhinandan Varthaman is now working in civilian aviation, information about the exact routes he will fly, his schedule and other employment details has not been officially released. It would be wrong to assume a particular route or aircraft assignment until the airline provides that information.

The transition from fighter pilot to commercial pilot also involves regulatory requirements. Military flying experience does not automatically mean that an officer can immediately operate a passenger aircraft for an airline. Civil aviation regulations require appropriate licences, examinations, medical certification, aircraft-specific training and other checks before a pilot can operate commercial flights.

Reports about Abhinandan's transition indicate that the necessary process for moving from military aviation into civilian flying has been followed. His extensive experience as an IAF fighter pilot provides a strong aviation background, but airline operations involve different procedures and responsibilities from military fighter operations.

For many people, the most interesting part of the story is the contrast between Abhinandan's two careers. He became famous while flying one of India's older fighter aircraft in a high-pressure military situation. He is now moving into an industry where the focus is on transporting passengers safely and efficiently on scheduled regional routes.

His new role also shows how military aviation careers can continue after retirement. Former Air Force pilots can bring valuable experience to airlines, including knowledge of cockpit procedures, communication, navigation, weather assessment, emergency response and aviation discipline.

Abhinandan's story has remained closely associated with the events of February 27, 2019. During the aerial confrontation, his MiG-21 was lost and he ejected. He was subsequently captured and became the focus of intense diplomatic attention between India and Pakistan.

His return to India three days later attracted huge public attention. Images and videos of his arrival were widely circulated, and his distinctive moustache and composed manner became instantly recognizable. The Vir Chakra later formally recognized his gallantry during the operation.

Abhinandan continued serving in the Indian Air Force after the 2019 incident. His return to flying was closely watched at the time, and reports from 2019 said he wanted to get back into the cockpit as soon as possible following his medical treatment.

Years later, he has now chosen another cockpit, this time in the commercial aviation sector. The FLY91 appointment gives him an opportunity to continue flying after leaving active military service.

The news has generated considerable interest in India because Abhinandan is not an ordinary former fighter pilot in the public imagination. His name is strongly associated with one of the most tense India-Pakistan military confrontations of recent decades.

At the same time, his new job should be viewed as a professional career transition rather than an extension of his military role. FLY91 is a civilian airline, and commercial aviation has its own operating standards, regulatory framework and passenger-safety requirements.

There has also been interest in what aircraft Abhinandan Varthaman may fly at FLY91. The airline operates ATR 72-600 turboprop aircraft on its regional network. However, the company has not publicly confirmed the exact aircraft or routes that Abhinandan will operate, so specific claims should be treated cautiously.

FLY91 serves regional destinations and is designed to connect cities that may not have extensive direct air links. Its operations from Goa and Hyderabad make it part of India's expanding regional aviation market.

The aircraft, mission and operating environment are very different, but both careers demand precision, training, teamwork and strict attention to safety.
