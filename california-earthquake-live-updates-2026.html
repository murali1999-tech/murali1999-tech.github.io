Why Earthquakes Frequently Occur in California

California experiences thousands of earthquakes every year due to the movement of tectonic plates beneath the Earth's surface. The state sits along major fault systems including the San Andreas Fault and San Jacinto Fault.

When tectonic plates shift or grind against one another, energy is released suddenly in the form of seismic waves. These waves create shaking that people feel as earthquakes.

Southern California, including Riverside and San Bernardino counties, is considered one of the most seismically active regions in the United States. Small earthquakes are common and help scientists study underground geological activity.

Experts say clusters of smaller earthquakes sometimes occur naturally as pressure is redistributed beneath fault systems. However, scientists cannot reliably predict exactly when larger earthquakes will occur.

Residents are encouraged to stay informed through official earthquake monitoring systems and emergency alerts.

Earthquake Safety Tips for California Residents

Earthquake preparedness is extremely important for people living in California. Emergency experts recommend keeping a disaster supply kit with food, water, flashlights, batteries, first aid items, medications, and important documents.

During an earthquake, remember the safety rule:

Drop, Cover, and Hold On.

Move away from windows, glass, heavy furniture, and unstable objects. If indoors, stay inside until shaking stops. If outside, move away from buildings, trees, power lines, and bridges.

Drivers should pull over safely and avoid stopping beneath overpasses or power lines.

After an earthquake, check for gas leaks, damaged electrical wiring, and structural damage. Continue monitoring emergency alerts for aftershocks and updated safety information.
